### 4.2 Does "selection-dominated" agree with the field? — yes, in direction and in size

* **Direction.** DriveSuprim Tab. 1: perfect selection among the top-4 by the model's own ranking removes 56 % of the NC×DAC zero proxy (Z 2.29 to 1.00) and among the top-16 78 %. CLOVER Tab. 5: the 64 proposals already contain a safe, high-PDMS trajectory almost always (Oracle@64 0.9933 to 0.9976), so the loss is the pick. TrajHF v2: oracle selector 94.3 vs deployable 87.6. TOAD states the opposite thesis for weak sets ("the candidate set, not the scorer, often limits performance") and then shows its search adds +0.1 on the two strongest scorers.
* **Size.** Our PROP share is 12.6 to 14.0 % of zeros (`D-REFE-ZEROTAIL-1/-2`), i.e. **0.52 to 0.58 PDMS** of the 4.15 is proposal-side at most (DERIVED: 4.15 × 0.126 to 0.140). Our best-of-64 (98.16) is 1.2 to 1.6 below the field's Oracle@64 (99.33 to 99.76); our selection gap (9.88) is **1.8 to 2.0× theirs** (5.0 to 5.6). The field is selection-bound too, but at half our gap.
* **Caveat.** The field's selection gap shrinks with scorer quality (Z 2.8 % → 1.4 %); none of these papers separates "scorer did not see it" from "scorer saw it and was wrong". ZT3's within-token AUC 0.46 (blind) and SELRULE-FIT's median p_DAC 0.93 (confident) point to the first on our arm.

### 4.4 The single highest-reach experiment I recommend (a proposal; needs its own pre-registration and the launch gate)

**P6-HN: add an expert-proximate near-twin hard-negative bank to the scorer loss, as a single-lever A/B against P6.** ESTIMATED reach **0.55 to 0.94 PDMS (13 to 23 % of 4.15)**, arithmetic in the row above; it is the only lever in the table whose published form (a) matches a mechanism we MEASURED on this arm (ZT3's ON_PATH near-twin grazes, 1.6 PDMS gross, DAC head blind), (b) has three independent same-sign deltas, and (c) costs one CPU label build plus one flag.

* **Step 0, 0 GPU:** census the existing on-policy labelled sets for near-twin pairs (lateral gap ≤ 0.5 m, same manoeuvre, one DAC = 0 and one DAC = 1). If pairs are sparse per set, the bank is where the signal is missing. Label cost is small: 103,039 scenes × 8 negatives is about 0.044 CPU-s per scene at c = 0.35 (DERIVED), under an hour on 8 workers.
* **Step 1, bank:** offsets of the teacher or GT path by about ±{0.15, 0.30, 0.45, 0.60} m, labelled by the landed `label_version 3` simulation. As in DA-WAM, hard negatives **enter the score loss only**: no WTA assignment, no gradient to the generator (the DrivoR detach), and they never enter the pick.
* **Step 2, arm:** P6 vs P6 + HN on the same seed pair; read with the ZT3/ZT4 package (zeros, near-twin accuracy), the four metric families, paired log-cluster bootstrap, **and a replicate**, because a separated interval from one seed answers only another draw of logs (`H-ESTIM-SEED-1`).
* **Both outcomes committed before the arm (Rule Zero 4):** HN clears (zeros fall with a paired CI excluding 0 and near-twin accuracy rises) → keep and stack iPad-style per-timestep targets as the next arm; HN does not → the blindness is representational, which sends the next arm to the trunk (P7.1 / S1d), not the loss.
* ⚠️ **Do not fold per-timestep targets into the same arm**: two levers in one arm is the `--v2` conflation failure. ⚠️ **Do not run it before P6 reads**: its published deltas sit on bases that already train the scorer end to end.

### 4.5 Why none of this is a forecast

Every published delta is one run and none has an interval; the borrowed σ is 0.41 pp from one base, and the only deltas above 3 σ are the weak-base ones. The bases differ in backbone, data, vocabulary and input. The conversion κ assumes a rescued token scores like the average (0.953 PDMS per pp), while rescued tokens come from harder scenes (turns are 33.6 % of tokens and 54.8 % of zeros), so κ is an upper estimate. Levers acting on the same blind head overlap. The honest statement is a range, 0.55 to 0.94, gross.
