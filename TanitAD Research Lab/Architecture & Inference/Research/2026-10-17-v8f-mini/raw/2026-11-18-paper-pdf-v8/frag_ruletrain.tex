\subsection{The rule in training, and the lead as an input}\label{sec:ruletrain}
The distance rule works at inference. Can the model learn to keep the distance itself? Every read of this subsection is \tier{T1-kin} (kinematic rollout on the model's own actions, logged frames, a lead that does not react; 642 lead windows of 56 episodes for the distance cells, 2,399 windows of 141 episodes for the cost cells; paired on windows, two-level bootstrap over bases and then episodes, $B=2000$), and every arm is a fine-tune from the two banked bases with a control of the same recipe and a rerun of the control as the floor. For reference: the label-fed rule at inference on the control reads $-0.0676$ $[-0.1008,-0.0362]$ at a position error of $+0.18$\,m, and the vision-fed margin rule $+0.86$ to $+0.95$\,m (\cref{tab:vrule}).

\paragraph{The rule in the training target (RULETRAIN, card ADD32 with note N1).} For samples with a label lead the logged future is replaced by its rule-capped form, with no rule at inference. At 2,000 steps the unsafe rate does not move (GRT minus control $+0.0000$ $[-0.0187,+0.0164]$, floor 0.0036: RULE-TRAINED-FLAT) and nothing costs (RULE-TRAINED-NO-COST); the share of lead windows whose plan the rule still changes at the first tick is unchanged (0.240/0.246 to 0.233/0.238), so by the card the dose is too small. With three times the steps (6,000) the result is the same (GRT6 minus control $-0.0053$ $[-0.0281,+0.0119]$, floor 0.0071; the two bases disagree in sign: RULE-TRAINED-FLAT-6K, RULE-TRAINED-NO-COST-6K), and with a weight of 4 on the changed samples it is flat again ($-0.0125$ $[-0.0417,+0.0067]$, floor 0.0071). The plan does respond to the dose: the gap bias at 2\,s rises from $+0.066$\,m to $+0.148$\,m $[+0.091,+0.216]$ to $+0.360$\,m $[+0.205,+0.543]$ against $+0.441$\,m for the rule at inference, and the unsafe rate falls by less than a fifth of what the rule achieves. In words: the relabelled target moves the samples where the logged driver came closer than the rule allows, while the unsafe steps of this tier sit on the windows where the model's own plan runs into the lead. A mean shift is not the lever; where the shift lands is. Training from the start with the relabel was not run.

\paragraph{The rule on the model's own plan (RULEOWN, card ADD34 with note N1).} A training term pulls a plan that runs into a label lead back onto its rule-capped form, with no rule and no lead input at inference. The squared form at weight 1 lowers U by $-0.0276$ $[-0.0647,+0.0089]$ (both bases below 0, the interval includes 0: OWN-RULE-FLAT) and costs LONGITUDINAL and TACTICAL cells (OWN-RULE-COSTS; 6\,s position error $+2.67$\,m $[+1.83,+3.55]$); weight 4 cuts U by $-0.0525$ $[-0.0973,-0.0116]$ at $+3.86$\,m $[+2.20,+5.58]$ and with LATERAL cells worse as well. The linear form (metres instead of squared metres) gives $-0.0409$ $[-0.0919,+0.0011]$ at $+2.12$\,m $[+1.34,+2.96]$ at weight 1 (OWN-RULE-L1-FLAT, OWN-RULE-L1-COSTS) and $-0.0578$ $[-0.1045,-0.0152]$ at $+3.29$\,m at weight 4. The bar OWN-RULE-L1-BEATS-MARGIN is \emph{not met} (price above $+0.86$\,m in both bases). The term does what it is built to do (fewer plans run into the rule at the first tick: 0.240/0.246 to 0.139/0.153 in the linear form) and the model pays with a general slowdown, also where no lead is involved: it cannot tell from its state precisely enough when to brake, so it brakes a little everywhere. A model that falls behind the logged driver has fewer unsafe steps for a trivial reason, which is why the cost cells are read beside U. The arms are not promoted.

\paragraph{The lead read as an input of the plan head (LEADCOND, LEADSCR, PLANLEAD).} The hypothesis was that the plan head cannot place its braking because the lead is not in its input. Adding the outputs of the supervised lead head (presence, gap) to the ego token after a fine-tune of 6,000 steps changes nothing beyond a rerun (LEAD-INPUT-FLAT: U $-0.017$ $[-0.032,-0.003]$ against a floor of $-0.019$ $[-0.035,-0.005]$; no cost), and with the own-plan term the input does not help it place its braking (LEAD-INPUT-NO-PLACEMENT: position error $+0.20$\,m $[-0.22,+0.62]$ against the term alone); the term cuts U on the LS base at the known price (TERM-LS-CUTS-UNSAFE $-0.055$ $[-0.092,-0.025]$, $+1.48$\,m; with input $-0.063$ $[-0.098,-0.032]$, $+1.67$\,m $[+1.07,+2.33]$, LEAD-INPUT-TERM-REACHES-ORACLE, LEAD-INPUT-TERM-COSTS), and the bar LEAD-INPUT-BEATS-MARGIN is \emph{not met}. The control q3 of that card is not met as written (it named the wrong field); the names are given with the corrected control q3b of note N1. Trained from the start the model does not use the input either (LEAD-INPUT-SCRATCH-UNUSED: zeroing the layer changes U by $-0.001$ $[-0.007,+0.005]$; LEAD-INPUT-SCRATCH-FLAT $-0.004$ $[-0.019,+0.013]$; LEAD-INPUT-SCRATCH-NO-COST). At the start of a lead window neither the label lead nor the model's own camera read explains the plan's speed error (PLAN-ERROR-NOT-LEAD: $+0.013$ $[-0.008,+0.034]$ and $-0.007$ $[-0.023,+0.009]$ of explained variance), so another place of entry for the lead read has nothing to gain there and that build is set aside. The control d4 of that read is not met as written; the name is given under d4b of a note written after the values were seen. What lowers the unsafe rate is what is told the model's own gap: the rule at inference.
