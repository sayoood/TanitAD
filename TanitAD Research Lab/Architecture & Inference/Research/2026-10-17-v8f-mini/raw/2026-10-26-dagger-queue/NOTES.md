## 3. THE GUARANTEE the gate's token and the training argv are the same bytes
The bank's sha256 exists only after the bank runs, and the token binds the argv sha256. So:
1. `gatedag` (on box 2) computes `sha256(bank.json)` with `v8f_dagger_queue_tool.py sha`, builds the TRAINING line, **writes it to `%RUNSM%\dagpin\M2b_s{S}_{arm}.train.argv.txt`** (the pin; a pin that already exists and differs is REFUSED, never replaced), and runs the gate on that line with `--out`/`--selfcheck-only` changed.
2. `traindag` re-derives the training line from the CURRENT bank.json, **`fc /b`-compares it to the pin (refuses on any difference)**, then executes the line **read from the pin file**, not the re-derived one.
3. The chain pulls the pin and `selfcheck.json`, requires pin == the line the chain itself PLANNED (a dry run of the queue with `DAGSHA_DRY` = the sha256 of the PULLED bank.json; `DAGSHA_DRY` is read only in a dry run: a selftest check and a mutant prove a real run ignores it), writes the token with `argv_sha256` of the pin, and after training requires `argv.json == pin == plan` (`DAG run record`).
MEASURED on the fake box (stub trainer, real cmd.exe, real queue, real shard tool): the argv the trainer RECEIVED in training == the pinned tokens == the gate's (modulo `--out`/`--selfcheck-only`); deliberate regressions that must be REFUSED and were: one byte changed in the pin (`--steps 3000`->`3001`, and `--lr`); `bank.json` changed by one byte after the gate; re-gating after the bank changed; `traindag` without a gate; `evaldag` before training.
