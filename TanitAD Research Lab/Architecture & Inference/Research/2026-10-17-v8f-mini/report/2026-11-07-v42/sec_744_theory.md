**7.44.2 T1 and T1′: what a squared-error planner does with unresolved intent (math, then [H]).**
* **Lemma 1 (the conditional mean).** For a target $Y$ with finite second moment and any function $f$ of the inputs $Z$,
$$
\mathbb{E}\big[(Y - f(Z))^{2}\big] \;=\; \mathbb{E}\big[\mathrm{Var}(Y \mid Z)\big] \;+\; \mathbb{E}\big[(\mathbb{E}[Y \mid Z] - f(Z))^{2}\big],
$$
so the minimiser is $f^{\star}(Z) = \mathbb{E}[Y \mid Z]$ (add and subtract $\mathbb{E}[Y\mid Z]$ inside the square; the cross term has zero mean). A planner trained under squared error therefore approximates a conditional mean, whatever its architecture. Wherever $Z$ does not resolve what the driver does next, the mean of the futures consistent with $Z$ is a blend of them.
* **T1, the level form [H] (REFUTED).** Let $g(v) = \mathbb{E}[v^{+} \mid v, z] = v - \delta(v, z)$ be the planner's speed return map, with $\delta > 0$ on the share of states where the logged future holds a deceleration the features cannot predict. Open loop the history is the human's, so one step of bias does not compound. Under T1-kin and T2 the planner's own executed speed becomes its history, so the speed follows $v_{k+1} = g(v_k)$, which converges to the fixed point $v^{\ast}$ with $\delta(v^{\ast}) = 0$ at rate $|g'(v^{\ast})| < 1$. **Prediction P1** (frozen): $g$ lies below the identity over 3 to 8 m/s with a fixed point below the human's median speed; P10's map lies nearer the identity. **Frozen failure condition:** *C0C's g(v) ≥ v over the band*.

* **T1′, the acceleration form [H] (restated after P1; Amendment 1, frozen before any P1′ measurement).** Let $A = (v^{+} - v)/H$ be the mean acceleration over the horizon $H = 2$ s and let $I \in \{\mathrm{go}, \mathrm{stay}\}$ be the driver's latent intent with posterior $\pi(z) = P(I = \mathrm{go} \mid z)$. Lemma 1 on the acceleration gives the mixture shrinkage
$$
\hat a(z) \;=\; \pi(z)\, a_{\mathrm{go}}(z) \;+\; \big(1 - \pi(z)\big)\, a_{\mathrm{stay}}(z), \qquad a_{I}(z) = \mathbb{E}[A \mid z, I].
$$
With $a_{\mathrm{stay}} \approx 0$ (hold) and $a_{\mathrm{go}}$ the human's acceleration, the shrinkage ratio is $\rho = \hat a / a_{\mathrm{human}} \approx \pi(z) \in [0, 1]$: **the car still accelerates, but less than the human**. Two exact consequences. (i) For a two-point intent the planner's irreducible squared error is $\mathbb{E}[\pi(1 - \pi)]\,(a_{\mathrm{go}} - a_{\mathrm{stay}})^{2}$ plus within-intent variance, so the same ambiguity that makes the mean useful makes it shrink. (ii) If $p_0 = P(\mathrm{go})$ and the go windows have constant $a_{\mathrm{go}}$, then the expected ratio on go windows is
$$
\mathbb{E}[\rho \mid \mathrm{go}] \;=\; \mathbb{E}[\pi(Z) \mid \mathrm{go}] \;=\; \frac{\mathbb{E}[\pi(Z)^{2}]}{p_0} \;=\; p_0 + \frac{\mathrm{Var}\,\pi(Z)}{p_0},
$$
using $\mathbb{E}[\pi \mathbb{1}_{\mathrm{go}}] = \mathbb{E}[\pi^{2}]$. The shrinkage is therefore governed by how far the features spread the posterior from the base rate: an uninformative $z$ gives $\pi \equiv p_0$ and a uniform shrinkage by $p_0$, a perfectly informative one gives $\pi \in \{0, 1\}$ and none. Over $H$ the speed shortfall on go windows is $H\,(1 - \bar\pi_{\mathrm{go}})(a_{\mathrm{go}} - a_{\mathrm{stay}})$ with $\bar\pi_{\mathrm{go}} = \mathbb{E}[\pi \mid \mathrm{go}]$. Consistent with this in sign and order, and post hoc: at T2 C0C's band map sits below the human's own (+1.68 [1.37, 2.02] m/s per 2 s) by −1.25 [−1.69, −0.83] at s0 and −0.62 [−1.30, +0.20] at s1, and at T1-kin with the human's history the plan minus the logged is −0.46 [−0.78, −0.11] / −0.49 [−0.80, −0.14] m/s per 2 s, separated at both seeds (**[M]**, `P1B_RESULT.json`, `P1C_RESULT.json`; **not findings**, they need the pre-registration below).

**7.44.3 T2: the data-processing inequality bounds every lever downstream of the features, and the ROC frontier follows (math; the tightness is [H]).**
* **Setting.** Let $I$ be the intent, $X$ the scene and history, $Z = \varphi(X)$ the features the planner reads, and $\hat Y = f(Z, \xi)$ any downstream function of $Z$ with independent randomness $\xi$: a retrained head, a re-weighting, DAgger on the same features, a floor, a forced level, or a weight edit of O or T. These form a Markov chain $I \to X \to Z \to \hat Y$.
* **Theorem (data processing).** $I(I; \hat Y) \le I(I; Z) \le I(I; X)$. For the binary decision $\hat c = \mathbb{1}\{\hat Y = \mathrm{go}\}$ the operational form is the Neyman–Pearson lemma: among all tests of false-go rate $\alpha = P(\hat c = 1 \mid \mathrm{stay})$, none has go-recall $\beta = P(\hat c = 1 \mid \mathrm{go})$ above that of the likelihood-ratio test on $Z$,
$$
\beta \;\le\; \beta^{\star}_{Z}(\alpha), \qquad \Lambda(z) = \frac{p(z \mid \mathrm{go})}{p(z \mid \mathrm{stay})}.
$$
(Proof: for the test $\phi^{\star} = \mathbb{1}\{\Lambda > t\}$ and any $\phi$ of equal size, $\int (\phi^{\star} - \phi)(p_{\mathrm{go}} - t\,p_{\mathrm{stay}})\,dz \ge 0$ because $\phi^{\star} = 1$ exactly where the bracket is positive.) The ROC curve of $\Lambda$ is concave and dominates the ROC of every other score $s(Z)$, so $\mathrm{AUC}(s) \le \mathrm{AUC}(\Lambda)$.
* **Mapping to the crawl [H].** Closing the crawl means answering "go" on more of the windows where the human went (go-recall $\beta$ up); the guard rows (DK-VIOL, TAC-LON, ADE6 at stops) measure answering "go" where the human stayed (false-go $\alpha$ up). Every lever that is a function of $Z$ therefore moves along one frontier, trading crawl closure for guard cost, and **a DOMINANT needs a point on the frontier with BOTH the M1 closure and intact guards**. The mapping of the report's rows onto $(\alpha, \beta)$ is a hypothesis (the rows are not exactly ROC coordinates); the inequality is a theorem.
* **How tight is it here?** The probe AUCs of F2 are the AUC of one function of $Z$, hence **lower bounds** on $\mathrm{AUC}(\Lambda)$; they are not a ceiling. What supports reading them as close to one is that three structurally different read-outs (a ridge, an MLP, a learned attention read-out on a finer grid) stop at the same place (§7.31, §7.33). That support is **[H]**. As an illustration only, **[E]** under an equal-variance binormal model, $\beta(\alpha) = \Phi\big(d' - \Phi^{-1}(1 - \alpha)\big)$ with $d' = \sqrt{2}\,\Phi^{-1}(\mathrm{AUC})$: at AUC 0.785 a false-go rate of 5 % buys a go-recall of 0.30, and at AUC 0.680 it buys 0.16. The post hoc line fit of §7.36.6 puts the closure that would stop the composite failing D2 on M1 near 0.68 / 0.60 of the excess **[E]**; at those operating points the frontier would not leave the guards intact.
* **Corollaries.** (1) **Lever ranking.** Only a lever that changes $\varphi$ (LoRA, motion features, more frames) or adds a channel can move the frontier; downstream levers can only choose a point on it. This is the formal form of "the lever is perception". (2) **Ceiling arms leave the chain.** The ceiling arms of §7.40 and §7.41 take the human's logged route speed, which is not a function of $Z$; they are inadmissible at inference and the theorem does not bind them. (3) **A weight edit of O or T is a function of $Z$** and lands on the frontier (§7.44.6).
* **P3 (frozen) [H]:** every banked downstream lever with a measured (M1 closure, guard) pair lies on or below one monotone frontier; the candidates are LF, A4 to A6, `lonfloor` and the boxes. **Frozen failure condition:** *one dominates another on both axes by more than its interval*.

**7.44.4 T3: the scope of LoRA on q and v with a fixed 4×10 pool [H].**
* **Parameter count and identity at initialisation.** The adapter adds $\Delta W = (\alpha/r)\,B A$ to $W_q$ and $W_v$ of each of the 24 blocks, with $A \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times d}$, $B \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times r}$, $d = 1024$, $r = 32$ and $B = 0$ at initialisation, so the encoder starts equal to the frozen one (the gate proves it: relative L2 0.0 on the pod, §7.43). The trainable count is $24 \times 2 \times (2 d r) = 3{,}145{,}728$. Each $\Delta W$ has rank at most 32.
* **What it can do.** In block $\ell$, head $h$, token $i$ attends $o_i = \sum_j \mathrm{softmax}_j(q_i^{\top} k_j / \sqrt{d_h})\, v_j$. LoRA on $q$ changes where each token looks and LoRA on $v$ changes what is carried, so it can raise the share of a cue the frozen pooling averages away: a light state, the lead vehicle's state, lane occupancy near the ego. These are single-frame cues.
* **What it cannot do.** The encoder is per-frame, $z_t = \varphi_\theta(x_t)$, so the held ages enter the planner as $(\varphi_\theta(x_{t-k}), \dots, \varphi_\theta(x_t))$ and **no term of the encoder couples two frames**. Motion must come from O and T differencing tokens across held ages, at the resolution of the 4×10 pool; F2 measured that the frozen read-out carries none (v WEAK, dv ABSENT, §7.38). LoRA can only add motion information if one frame contains a cue that is monotone in speed (blur, the lead's pose against the lane), and it cannot create motion inside one frame.
* **Prediction P4 (frozen) [H].** *the go/stay AUC on L's features rises over V+E on light and lead classes before it rises on the pure self-motion class*. It sits beside the card's M-LAT and decides nothing. **State: NOT EVALUABLE, no L checkpoint exists (§7.43).** Its scope under T2: LoRA changes $\varphi$, so it is allowed to move the frontier, but not past $I(I; X)$; and the card's own prior for a PRIMARY pass is LOW **[E]**.

**7.44.5 T4 and the weight-space method: what a weight edit can and cannot do, what the weights say (math, [P], [M]).**
* **[P] The method.** Takehi, Teoh, Zhang and Khattab (2026-11-02, *Sparks of Weight-Space Programming*, a blog post, not peer-reviewed; Library key `takehi2026weightspace`; the page is banked under `R/raw/2026-11-06-theory-crawl/library/`, sha256 `a326bc5b…`, and the Library index entry is not on the tip) report that an LLM read a 7.16M-parameter network's circuits from its weights with Python analysis and no forward pass, that its claims matched ablation ground truth, and that its strongest edit subtracted one direction from 64 output columns in two MLPs and removed an obstacle (reasoning loops). The authors state that an edit removes an obstacle and does not add an ability (a Gemma 3 270M math edit failed). **Descriptions here follow the banked page; none of it is a result of ours.**
* **Our protocol.** (1) State claims from the tensors alone and hash them before any forward pass (`CLAIMS_P2.md`, sha256 `afb9785f…`, stamped 22:14:27Z). (2) Check each claim by Jacobian (autograd against finite differences) and by input ablation on held-out windows (eval-141, tick 0, 586 windows in 70 episodes at 3 to 8 m/s, C0 s0, replicated at s1). (3) Only then edit.
* **What the weights can and cannot give (math).** For $y = W_L\,\sigma(\cdots \sigma(W_1 x))$ the Jacobian at the input $x$ is $W_L D_{L-1} W_{L-1} \cdots D_1 W_1$ with $D_\ell = \mathrm{diag}\,\sigma'(\text{pre-activation}_\ell)$. The weights fix the factors $W_\ell$; **the data enter only through the operating point $D_\ell$** (and, in attention, through the softmax weights). A weight-only claim is therefore a statement at an assumed $D$: the linear limit ($D = I$) or a bracket $D \in [0, \sup \sigma']$. This is exactly where the two weight-only claims that failed failed (below).
* **What an edit can do (math).** A rank-one edit $\Delta W = u\,w^{\top}$ of a linear layer $y = W h + b$ gives $(W + \Delta W)h = W h + u\,(w^{\top} h)$: the output moves along $u$ by the projection of $h$ on $w$. If $h = h(z)$ is a function of the planner's inputs, **the edited network is still a function of $Z$**, so by §7.44.3 it cannot add the intent information F2 says is missing. It can remove an obstacle: a component of the learned adjustment that is not information-bearing, such as an unconditional "regress to the mean". Its value is diagnostic (where the shrinkage lives in the weights) and it adds a frontier point at zero training cost.
