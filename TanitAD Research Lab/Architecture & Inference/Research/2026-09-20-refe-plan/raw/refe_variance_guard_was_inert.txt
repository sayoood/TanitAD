MY OWN GUARD WAS INERT, AND A DOC I WROTE CITED IT AS PROOF. MEASURED 2026-09-20.

THE CLAIM I MADE (REFE_MODEL.md section 8.3):
  'All six components discriminate, and train.py PROVES it rather than assuming it.'
  'MEASURED over 664 rows: off_road sd 0.468, collision 0.412, ttc 0.375, goal_reaching
   0.294, comfort 0.155, center_line 0.111 -- none inert.'

IT IS WRONG. The guard pooled EVERY ROW IN THE BANK and reported one standard deviation per
component. A component that is CONSTANT across the candidates of every single frame, and
merely differs BETWEEN frames, shows a healthy pooled spread and passes -- while being
completely unable to rank anything. The scorer's job is to choose among the candidates OF ONE
FRAME, so the only measurement that answers the question is the spread WITHIN a frame.

THE CORRECTED GUARD, run on the live bank (5,573 rows over 620 frames, 4 shards):
  component        mean  pooled sd  varies in
  off_road        0.720      0.449      99.8 %
  collision       0.625      0.484      75.5 %
  ttc             0.614      0.475      86.8 %
  comfort         0.969      0.120       0.0 %   *** CANNOT RANK
  goal_reaching   0.910      0.286      52.3 %
  center_line     0.825      0.108      76.8 %

⛔ comfort VARIES IN 0.0 % OF FRAMES. Its pooled sd of 0.120 is larger than center_line's
  0.108, which DOES rank in 76.8 % of frames -- so the pooled number does not even order the
  components correctly. One of the six is contributing nothing to proposal ranking, and the
  instrument built to detect exactly that was blind to it.

WHY IT MATTERS BEYOND THE ONE COMPONENT. The paper's scoring loss is a sum over all six
  components, and at inference DriveZero selects the candidate with the highest aggregate.
  A component that is constant within a frame adds a per-frame bias to every candidate
  equally: it cannot change the argmax, so a sixth of the scoring head is doing no work.

ROOT-CAUSE CLASS: a check whose QUESTION is narrower than the claim hung on it -- the same
  family as the four self-referential checks of 2026-09-07 and as the episode-cluster
  bootstrap being blind to training variance. The estimator was fine; it was answering
  'do these numbers differ anywhere' when the claim was 'can this rank candidates here'.

⭐ WHAT CAUGHT IT: not a re-reading. An INDEPENDENT reviewer measured within-frame variation
  directly (0 of 540 frames on its snapshot) and named the guard as the reason it had
  survived. I then reproduced it with the corrected guard on a larger bank: 0.0 % of 620.

Instrument: refe/train.py ScorerBank.variance_report, now reporting 'varies in <frac> % of
frames' and naming anything under 5 % as CANNOT RANK.
