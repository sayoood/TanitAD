# L1 (two-sided lateral repair of the teacher target): navtrain development, 2026-11-13

Evidence class for every number: MEASURED (ours), navtrain, builder scorer (W-TRANSP nuPlan-DB rebuild, tracked), tier T1 builder-side. Arm naming: "RJ5 teacher + P5-R + REPAIR-D" (A1) and "RJ5 teacher + P5-R + L1". No navtest token was scored or read. CPU only, BLAS threads 1, at most 2 workers.

## What was built
- `n4_repair_l1.py` (sha256 `7af0d3dd52becb63...`, hold copy only): `repair_l1(cx, t20, base, ...)` with the call shape of `n4_repair.repair_target`; imports the pinned `n4_repair` / `n4_geom`, copies nothing. Same certificate (`n4_repair.certified`), same margin 0.05 m, cap 5.
- Per iteration: label -> score -> certificate; else per-step (k = 1..40) feasible lateral interval of all four footprint corners inside the drivable union eroded by the margin (0.01 m grid, +-1.5 m); window-intersect to the 20 target poses; pull the approached side `centre_m` = 0.05 m deeper (capped at half the width); minimum-total-variation ramp from t0 inside the bounds (LP, HiGHS); step along the executed-heading left normal; halve the step on a sign flip of the dominant correction; INFEASIBLE flag when a step has no feasible offset even at the fallback margin 0.005 m (relaxed steps are recorded).
- Parameters were set BEFORE any navtrain failure set existed and were not tuned: margin 0.05, cap 5, centre 0.05, fallback margin 0.005, damp 0.5 (non-cumulative), grid 0.01, dmax 1.5, eps 0.01.
- `test_n4_repair_l1.py`: 8 passed (`test_run.log`): synthetic two-sided corridor certifies, zero correction is bit-identical (label equals the base label, `PASSED_AT_0`), passing label returned unchanged at iteration 0, sign-flipped regression arm does not certify (synthetic AND on two real failing navtrain instances), infeasible flag, certificate-blocked path, reachability (parameters read back from the built partial equal the ones used; margin and iters change behaviour, not only the read-back), determinism (two runs bit-identical).

### Controls and facts behind the table
- A1 control: 30 of 30 certified, matching the banked `Q3_repairs.json` count; the repaired plans differ from the banked ones by at most 8.2e-4 m (max abs pose difference; the banked run came from a different builder tree/environment), so reproduction is by count and certificate, NOT bit-identical. INHERITED: no cause established.
- 3a: dual labeller (`p5c_dual_labels.py`, unchanged, lambda* 3.0, 2 shards, 1,884 of 1,884 rows labelled, 0 errors) then builder scoring: **1 of 1,884 rows has tracked DAC 0** (token `9c3b385208ae5cda`), A1 and L1 both repair it. C-DERIV (builder `labels()` vs dual labeller P5-R) on all DAC-0 rows plus a sha1 5 % sample (98 rows): max deviation 0.0. Residual REPAIR-D failures among 1,884 rows: **0**. Rows only from batches with a `.done` file (0 and 1); batch 2 and later were not read.
- Navtrain corridors are wide: over the 1,883 DAC = 1 rows the median minimum lateral free width (beyond the footprint, at margin 0.05) is 3.0 m (capped at the 1.5 m grid), 1st percentile 1.5 m; 467 rows have the nearer boundary within 0.48 m, but of those only 15 also have the far boundary within 1.4 m and 6 within 1.0 m. So a naturally two-sided navtrain corridor essentially does not occur.
- B1 is therefore one-sided by construction in practice (reported as such). Its known feasible answer: the unshifted label has DAC = 1 (MEASURED at scan).
- B2 / B3: the corridor is made two-sided by eroding the scorer's map and `cx.drivable` by e = (wmin - slack) / 2 (median e 0.76 m and 0.95 m); each polygon is intersected with union.buffer(-e) so seams stay closed. The known feasible answer is an independent ORACLE, not L1: the target shifted per pose by the midpoint of the original free interval (ramp 1.0 s in B2, 0.2 s in B3), relabelled and scored on the eroded map; an instance is kept only if the oracle certifies (MEASURED per instance, `raw/stress_b*_out.jsonl.gz` field `oracle`). Most draws failed the oracle (60 / 100, 74 / 100), which is why the kept sets are small and why the true corridor-width limit of what REPAIR-D can do was not reached.
- A single-sided violation is repaired by A1 in one iteration on all 53 B2/B3 instances: A1 never ping-ponged on any construction (lateral sign flips >= 2: 0 of 53; iterations >= 3: 0 of 53). The wiggle variant (`--wiggle`, lateral sinusoid on the target) was tried on 8 tokens only and also produced no A1 failure; the label pipeline smooths the wiggle.
- Deliberate regression L1x (all shift signs flipped) repairs 0 of 30 + 1 + 45 + 30 + 23 = 129 instances: 0 certified (statuses INFEASIBLE or CAP_EXHAUSTED).

## What this means for the card (Rule Zero: not a verdict)
The ping-pong class seen on navtest (A1 residuals, sign flips >= 2 in 16 of 20 non-guard tokens, extents 0.03 to 0.15 m) is NOT reproduced by any navtrain data or by the constructions above; whether L1 repairs it is untested until a set that REPAIR-D fails exists. Named blockers and unblockers:
1. More RJ5 navtrain rows: the re-roll job (2 of 17 batches finished when read, batch 2 in progress) yields about 1 DAC-0 row per 1,900 rows and 0 REPAIR-D residuals so far; the expected count of residuals over the whole navtrain set is of order 0 to a few. Unblocker: the job finishes (it is running; no action needed) and this exact pipeline is re-run on the new rows (`n5_scan.py` -> `n5_run.py`).
2. A construction that actually makes REPAIR-D ping-pong (tried: B1-B3 and the wiggle variant; all repaired in one iteration). A cheaper route may be a corridor from the real residual geometry, which needs the navtest residual tokens (PI decision: those 25 are seen-on-navtest, so reading them to design L1 would make the N5 read non-clean).
3. PI decision: whether L1 may be run once, descriptively, on the 20 non-guard navtest residual tokens before the freeze (a feasibility read that cannot then be called out-of-sample for those 20), or whether N5 is frozen as is and navtest is the first discriminating test.
