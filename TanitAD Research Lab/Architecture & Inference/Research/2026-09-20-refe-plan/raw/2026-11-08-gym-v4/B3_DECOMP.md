## The reward in one line (MEASURED, `scorer_gym/gym_env.py:585-677`)
`reward_cycle = clip((ds - brake_ref)/(limit x 0.5 s), 0, 1.5) x prod(q_k)`. Progress is normalised by the LANE LIMIT. `q_free` applies only if no lead, no conflict agent with a projected hit within 4 s (`conf_ttc is None`) and `min(limit, curve_speed) >= 1.0`; it is `clip(1 - 0.7 (0.9 v* - vbar)/(0.4 v*), 0.3, 1)`, so the 0.3 floor is reached at vbar <= 0.5 v*.

## Answers
**1. Why does the crawl beat 0.3x and 0.5x on state 17? No reward term is responsible: the "crawl" is not slower than those rungs.** State 17's lane limit is 2.235 m/s (5 mph). The 0.3x and 0.5x rungs therefore target 0.67 and 1.12 m/s, the crawl targets 1.50 m/s = 0.67 x limit, and the 0.7x rung is 1.56 m/s. Progress is paid per metre, so the order of returns follows the order of speeds: sum progress 9.47 (crawl) vs 7.06 (0.5x) vs 4.32 (0.3x). Cycle detail (`b3_decomp.json`): crawl cycles 1-11 pay 0.27-0.64 each with qprod 1.0 except q_free 0.55-0.59 on cycles 5-7; 0.5x pays 0.20-0.48. The crawl even loses less to q_yield (4 cycles, 12, 14-16) than 0.5x (7 cycles, 9-15). The two crawl-specific leaks, MEASURED: (a) `q_free` is switched OFF on every cycle where a conflict agent's projected hit is within 4 s (`conf_ttc` 3.0-3.5 on crawl cycles 8-11) although v = 1.5 = 0.67 v*; (b) `q_yield` needs v > 1.0 m/s, so the 0.3x rung (0.67 m/s) is exempt from it. Neither explains the ordering. `q_stall` fired on cycles 1 and 2 of the **0.3x** rung (`attrib.json` stall_cycles `17:f0.3`), not on the crawl: v0 = 0.2 and the slow ramp keeps v < 0.5 for the first 2 s (v = 0.18, 0.36, 0.50), so the rule meant for a clear-junction stall bites a launch from rest. Cost 0.15 return units; irrelevant to B3 ordering.
**2. Which term must change for crawl/best <= 0.10 on 3, 11, 17 without touching 0.9x/1.0x?** Only `q_free` (its floor) can act, and only on state 3. Crawl on state 3 sits at the 0.3 floor on all 17 cycles, and progress is not the lever (prog/best 0.354 is set by speed/limit). Crawl/best = 0.354 x floor / 0.917 (best's mean q), so it needs floor <= 0.259. Exact counterfactual (floor transform, scripted rungs do not depend on the reward; `attrib.json floor_cf`): floor 0.25 -> 0.097; **0.20 -> 0.077**; 0.15 -> 0.058; 0.10 -> 0.039. State 11 is already 0.065 and falls to 0.044 at 0.20. State 17 is unmoved at every floor (0.447). The 0.9x/1.0x rungs are not strictly untouched, because their launch cycles also sit at the floor: at 0.20 they change by -0.24 %/-0.22 % (state 3) and -2.2 %/-2.1 % (state 11, a 13.4 m/s ramp); over the 10 hard-free 0.9x/1.0x cells of all 20 states the mean is -0.48 %, worst -2.2 % (`all20_floor_effect`). Caveat: the transform sets every floor-sitting cycle to the new floor; a cycle with vbar between 0.44 and 0.5 v* would really land between 0.2 and 0.3, so the cut is slightly overstated (the stated 0.9x/1.0x change is an upper bound; state-3 crawl is exact, vbar = 0.34 v*).
**3. Is 0.447 a reward defect or a property of state 17? A property of the state (a ladder-definition defect), not a reward defect.** Evidence: (i) the crawl runs at 0.67 x limit, 4 % slower than the 0.7x rung, which returns 0.514 x best; any reward monotone in speed prices the crawl within ~13 % of the 0.7x rung (6.39 vs 7.36 measured). A crawl <= 0.10 x best would force the 0.7x rung to ~0.11, i.e. price 70 % of the limit as a crawl; that cannot be wanted. (ii) The best rung is NOT limited by something ahead: 1.0x reaches 2.24 m/s with qprod 1.0 on 16 of 17 cycles and no hard event. (iii) The best reward-side case: re-applying q_free on crawl cycles 8-11 (v*= limit 2.24; ESTIMATED, curve speed not stored) removes about 1.0 return unit, giving about 0.38, still 3.8x the bar. B3 as written (crawl fixed at 1.5 m/s) is unsatisfiable on any state whose limit is below about 3.4 m/s for every floor >= 0.2: crawl/best ~ (1.58/limit) x floor/0.92. State 3 (limit 4.47) is the opposite case: a genuine q_free defect (floor too high), repairable.
