THE LR SCHEDULE REACHES EXACTLY ZERO. MEASURED 2026-09-20 on PI instruction.

WHAT WAS WRONG. train.py built `AdamW(lr=a.lr, weight_decay=1e-4)` with NO SCHEDULER AT ALL and a
default `--lr 3e-4`, against the paper's "an initial learning rate of 2 x 10^-4 decayed to zero via
cosine annealing". Both halves were UNINTENDED divergences -- nothing in our docs had justified
either -- and the PI instructed the fix directly.

WHAT CHANGED
  --lr default            3e-4 -> 2e-4
  scheduler               none -> CosineAnnealingLR(T_max=steps, eta_min=0.0)
  --weight-decay          exposed (was hardcoded 1e-4)
  --no-cosine             added, ablation only
  --epochs                added; DERIVES --steps as epochs * ceil(len(bank)/batch) BEFORE the
                          optimiser is built, so "25 epochs at batch 256" is expressible in its own
                          unit and the cosine T_max spans the whole run. Until now the trainer
                          counted steps only, so the paper's schedule could not be stated at all.
  live LR                 printed on every log line, so the decay is OBSERVED, not assumed

WHY THIS TEST IS NOT A TRAINING RUN. Verifying a schedule by training is expensive and answers the
wrong question: the 62-step confirmation run took 108 s for its FIRST step under load, and a loss
curve cannot separate "the scheduler is configured correctly" from "the model happened to improve".
The schedule is a property of the optimiser, so this tests the optimiser -- in under a second.

⭐ THE CONTROL IS THE --no-cosine ARM. A check that only asserts "the LR went down" passes on any
decaying schedule and on several wrong ones. The ablation must hold the LR FLAT at 2e-4, and it
does. If both arms decayed, the flag would do nothing and this test would be inert -- the same
failure mode as the four self-referential checks logged on 2026-09-07.
