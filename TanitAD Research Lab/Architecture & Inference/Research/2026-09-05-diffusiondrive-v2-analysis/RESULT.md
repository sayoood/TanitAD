## 0. The answer in one paragraph

DiffusionDriveV2 keeps DiffusionDrive's encoder byte-for-byte and its truncated-diffusion generator almost byte-for-byte, then adds **two training stages and one inference module**: (I) a 10-epoch, **head-only** RL post-train in which each anchor's 4-sample group is scored by the **NAVSIM PDM simulator** and pushed by a plain REINFORCE gradient under a truncated, GT-thresholded advantage; (II) a 20-epoch **coarse-to-fine selector** that predicts the five PDM sub-scores of 800 candidates and replaces v1's classifier entirely. Published gain: **88.1 → 91.2 PDMS** (NAVSIM v1) and **85.5 EPDMS** (NAVSIM v2 navtest), of which the authors' own control attributes **+1.0 to the selector and ≈ +2.1 to RL**, and the raw-fan floor (PDMS@10) moves **75.3 → 84.4** while diversity **falls** 42.3 → 30.3. The pinned code departs from the paper in nine places that matter for porting (§1.3): the RL decoder is **one** cascade layer, not v1's two; the "η = 1 DDPM" exploration is **only** a 4 % multiplicative scale noise (the additive term is multiplied by zero); the likelihood used for the gradient is an isotropic Gaussian with a constant σ = 0.1 that **does not match the sampler**; positive advantage is given only to samples that **beat the human's own PDM score**; the −1 branch fires on **drivable-area** failure as well as collision; the IL term pulls **all 80 samples** toward the GT; the v1 confidence head is **untrained and unused**; and the coordinate normalisation changed. For REF-C the consequence is sharp: **nothing in the RL stage requires a diffusion sampler in practice — the released method explores a two-parameter (longitudinal-scale, lateral-scale) family per anchor**, which is exactly the along-track axis on which our fan's deficit sits (`D-REFCV3-AXIS1`), so the cheapest faithful port is a scale-policy over refcv4's deterministic fan (E-DDA-3b), and the selector stage is portable today onto the emitted fan (E-DDA-2b). What does **not** port is the reward's map half (DAC, lane-keeping) — PhysicalAI has no map — and any reward that reads the GT future path is the IL loss wearing a reward costume (§3.3).

### 1.2 What V2 adds — mechanism by mechanism (paper `PUBLISHED-PRIMARY` ↔ code `PUBLISHED-CODE`)

**(a) The denoising policy and how a policy loss enters a diffusion decoder.** Paper Eq. 5: each denoising step is a Gaussian `π_θ(τ_{t−1} | τ_t) = N(μ_θ(τ_t, t, z, a_k), η(1−α_t)·I)`, η = 1 in training, 0 at eval; Eq. 6 is REINFORCE over the truncated chain. Code: `DDIMScheduler_with_logprob.step` (`:518-676`) computes the ordinary DDIM mean `prev_sample_mean = √ᾱ_{t−1}·x̂0 + √(1−ᾱ_{t−1}−σ_t²)·ε̂` (`:627-630`), draws the next sample (see (b)), and returns `log_prob = −(x_{t−1}.detach() − μ)²/(2σ'²) − log σ' − log√(2π)` **summed over the 8 × 2 coordinates** (`:668-675`). The gradient reaches the decoder through `μ`, i.e. through the decoder's x̂0 prediction at each of the **10** rollout steps. Two forward passes per batch: a `no_grad` rollout that samples, scores and builds advantages (`V2TransfuserModel.forward:258-259` → `forward_train_rl`), then `get_rlloss` (`:1028-1120`) re-runs the decoder **with gradient on the stored chain** (`prev_sample=chains_prev[...,i]`, `:1085-1091`) and forms `per_token_loss = −exp(logp − logp.detach()) · A` (`:1096`) — the ratio is identically 1, so this is **plain REINFORCE with one update per rollout**; there is **no importance ratio, no clip, no reference policy and no KL** anywhere in the file (grep: `kl`, `clip(` only on σ, `ref` none). The sibling's derivation (`INHERITED`, its finding #2: "DDPO_SF wearing GRPO's advantage") is **confirmed by the code**.

**(b) Scale-adaptive multiplicative exploration noise.** Paper §4.3: `τ' = (1 + ε_mul) ⊙ τ` with two scalars (longitudinal, lateral) per trajectory, so the explored path stays smooth; supplement: minimum denoising std **0.04**, minimum likelihood std **0.10**. Code (`:638-666`): `std_dev_t_mul = clip(σ_t, min=0.04)`; two `randn([B, N, 1, 1])` scalars, `(·)·σ_mul + 1`, broadcast over the 8 waypoints; **`std_dev_t_add = 0.0`** in both branches (`:640, :643`); `prev_sample = prev_sample_mean · ε_mul + 0 · ε_add` (`:666`). ⇒ The additive DDPM noise the paper's "η = 1" describes is generated and **multiplied by zero**; the only stochasticity in the RL chain is the 2-scalar multiplicative scale. `ESTIMATED` (`raw/ddv2_noise_magnitudes.json`): with the diffusers `scaled_linear` schedule, σ_t(η = 1) is **0.0083–0.0120** at every rollout timestep t ∈ {18, 16, …, 2} and 0 at t = 0, so **both floors bind everywhere** — exploration std is a constant **4 %** of the coordinate (0.40 m along-track at x = 10 m, 1.60 m at 40 m; 0.08 m lateral at y = 2 m) and the likelihood std is a constant **0.1 in normalised units = 5.0 m (x) / 2.0 m (y)**. The noise *schedule* is irrelevant in the RL stage; the sibling's "it is a FLOOR, not a schedule" (`INHERITED`) is confirmed and sharpened: the floor is the whole mechanism.

**(c) Groups, and the intra-anchor advantage.** Paper Eq. 8: `A^{k,i} = (r^{k,i} − mean_G) / std_G` within the G samples of anchor k. Code: `num_groups = 4` (`_rl_config.py:37`; `_sel_config.py` says 8 but the selector hard-codes its own counts, §1.2(i)); the anchor bank is tiled `[B, 4, 20, 8, 2]` (`:813-815`), fresh Gaussian noise at t = 8 per copy, so each anchor's group is 4 independent chains; `advantages = (r − mean_over_G) / (std_over_G + 1e-4)` (`:886-889`). The `std` divisor is present, as the sibling warned (`INHERITED`, "do not port the divisor without deciding it" — our `rl/advantage.py` defaults to centre-only). Discount: `0.8 ** (step_num − i − 1)` over the 10 steps, cleanest step weighted 1.0 (`:926-932`); paper γ = 0.8 ✔.

**(d) What "constrained" constrains — the inter-anchor truncated advantage.** Paper Eq. 10: `A_trunc = −1 if collision else max(0, A)`. Code (`:891-902`) does **three** things, two of them absent from the paper: (i) `mask_positive = reward_group > reward_gt − 1e-6` — **positive advantage only for samples whose PDM score is at least the ground-truth trajectory's own PDM score** (the GT is appended as the 81st candidate and scored with the rest, `:867-873`); (ii) `advantages.clamp(min=0) * mask_positive`; (iii) `−1` wherever the sub-score `no_collision != 1` **or `drivable_area != 1`** (`:900-902`) — the paper says collision only. TTC/comfort/progress sub-scores are read but do not modify the advantage (`:903-904`). So "constrained" means: *never push probability toward a mode that merely wins its group; push only toward samples that beat the human, and pull hard away from collision and off-road.* There is **no reference policy and no KL** — the paper's §4.4 sentence *"analogous to how the original GRPO provides regularization by adding a KL divergence … we incorporate an additional imitation learning loss"* is the entire trust-region story, and the code matches it.

**(e) The IL term inside the RL stage.** Paper Eq. 9: `L = L_RL + λ·L_IL`, λ = 0.1 (Tab. 7 "BC loss weight"). Code (`:1104-1118`): `IL_loss = mean L1(poses_reg[..., :2], GT)` over **all** `num_groups × 20 = 80` chains, all 10 timesteps and all decoder layers — i.e. **every mode, positive or not, is pulled toward the single GT**; and `il_weight` is **1.0 for any batch row with no positive-advantage sample, 0.1 otherwise** (`:1113-1117`). This is not v1's nearest-anchor-only L1 (`LossComputer`), which V2 instantiates but never calls in the RL stage. Note for our register: the sibling's E.2 objection to our library ("the imitation signal is counted twice — inside the reward and as a separate IL loss") describes **the published code as well** — the GT enters the reward twice (progress normalisation and the ≥ GT mask) *and* the IL term. A fact to report, not a licence.

**(f) The reward — the paper's biggest absence, settled by the code.** The paper never defines `R(τ_0)` (sibling finding #1, `INHERITED`, MEASURED-ABSENCE on 17 pages). Code: `TrajectoryHead.__init__` loads `navsim/planning/script/config/pdm_scoring/default_scoring_parameters.yaml` and instantiates NAVSIM's **`PDMSimulator` + `PDMScorer`** (`:741-753`), runs them in a 16-process pool (`_pdm_worker`, `:119-135`) on each sample's lzma-pickled metric cache, and `_pairwise_scores` (`:75-117`) rebuilds the PDM score **per candidate against the GT as proposal 0**: `final = NC × DAC × (5·EP + 5·TTC + 2·C + 0·DDC) / 12`, with `EP` re-normalised as `progress_i / max(progress_GT, progress_i)` (`:93-105`). Sub-scores returned: `no_collision, drivable_area, progress, ttc, comfort, dir_weighted, final` (`:66-74`). ⇒ **The reward is NAVSIM's PDM score from the non-reactive PDM simulator (LQR-tracked 4 s rollout at 10 Hz — `proposal_sampling: num_poses 40, interval 0.1`, replayed agents), and the `collision` predicate of Eq. 10 is NAVSIM's `NC` multiplied with `DAC`.** The sibling's "HYPOTHESIS, NOT ASSERTED — the reward is PDMS" is now `PUBLISHED-CODE`. ⚠️ The paper calls this *"closed-loop evaluation"*; NAVSIM's own title is *"non-reactive autonomous vehicle simulation"* (V2 ref. [10]) — under the PI's 2026-09-02 ruling (`VOCABULARY.md`, `open-vs-closed-loop-ruling`) a 4-s kinematic rollout against replayed agents is **self-action open loop (our T1 class)**, not closed loop. That matters in §3: their "closed-loop" reward is *our* tier.

**(g) Rollout length and inference.** Paper: T_trunc unspecified for RL; inference 2 steps. Code: RL rollout **`step_num = 10`**, `roll_timesteps = [18, 16, …, 2, 0]` (`:801-807`), while the noise is added at t = 8 (`:819`) — the chain's timestep labels exceed the actual noise level, the same asymmetry v1 has with `[10, 0]` from t = 8 (audit §1.1 #2), inherited unchanged; `prev_timestep = t − 1` each step (`:580-582`). Inference and the selector use 2 steps.

**(h) The mode selector.** Paper §4.6: trajectory coordinates as queries → deformable BEV attention → agent/map cross-attention → MLP scores; coarse-to-fine after DriveSuprim; BCE + margin-rank (Eq. 11, m unpublished). Code (`_model_sel.py`): candidate embedding = sinusoid of (x, y) at 64/pt **plus a heading channel at 32/pt from a Bézier-derived yaw** (`bezier_xyyaw`, `:1558-`; the generator's own heading output is discarded) → `plan_anchor_scorer_encoder` → **512-d** (`:827-830`, `:1140-1150`). `ScorerTransformerDecoderLayer` (`:396-477`): d = **512, 16 heads, FFN 2048, dropout 0.2**; grid-sample BEV attention (`GridSampleCrossBEVAttentionScorer`) → agent MHA → **self-attention among candidates** (`:435`, `:466` — the generator has none) → ego MHA → FFN. Coarse = **1 layer** + five heads `NC, EP, DAC, TTC, C` (`:853-873`); fine = **3 layers** + five separate heads (`:882-902`). Score = `σ(NC)·σ(DAC)·(5σ(TTC) + 5σ(EP) + 2σ(C))/12` — **the PDMS formula with predicted sub-scores** (`:1032-1037`, `:1461`). **Top-k = 32** (`:1372`, `:1469`). Losses: BCE per sub-score against the simulator's sub-scores (NC = 0.5 mapped to 0, comfort masked where −1; `:990-999`) + **`MarginRankingLoss(margin = 0.05)` on all EP pairs, weight 2** (`:904`, `:1005-1021`); fine loss averaged over its 3 layers; total = coarse + fine (`:1386`). Trainable at stage II: only the scorer encoder, the two scorer decoders and the ten heads (`_sel_agent.py:61-75`); generator frozen.

**(i) Selector training data and inference fan.** Per scene: 2 groups × 20 anchors (`:1298`), 2 DDIM steps at η = 0, then `add_mul_noise(n_aug=2, std ∈ U(0.1, 0.2))` (`:1346`) → 120 candidates, simulator-scored; plus a **GTRS vocabulary** slice — `16384.npy[:, ::5]` = 3,277 trajectories with precomputed PDM sub-scores from `navtrain_16384.pkl`, kept at `dropout_ratio = 0.99` ≈ 32 per scene (`:1352-1357`, `get_vocab_pdm_subscores:1202-1268`; the vocab's comfort is set to −1 and masked). Paper Tab. 7 "2 aug trajs, std (0.1, 0.2), 1 % of GTRS vocab" ✔. **At inference** (`forward_test_rl:1390-1523`): **10 groups × 20 anchors = 200** denoised candidates → `add_mul_noise()` default **n_aug = 3, std ∈ U(0.1, 0.3)** → **800 candidates** → coarse scorer → top-32 → fine scorer → the last fine layer's argmax is the emitted trajectory (`:1491` — the official-eval return is commented out; `run_pdm_score_fast.py` instead PDM-scores the coarse pick and the three fine-layer picks against the metric cache and reports their means, `:1493-1523`). ⚠️ `UNVERIFIED` which of the two eval paths produced Table 1's 91.2; `docs/train_eval.md` presents the fast path as *"several times faster than the official evaluation script"* and equivalent.

## 2. Results — `PUBLISHED-PRIMARY`, with the caveat that travels with each number

All numbers below are single-run and carry no interval in the primary (sibling §A.3, confirmed: no seed count or CI appears anywhere in the 17 pages). Backbone ResNet-34 (21.8 M) unless stated. ## 3. What transfers to REF-C under our constraints

Constraints applied to every row: vision-only at inference (no ego beyond the measured `v0`, no map, no LiDAR); no NAVSIM simulator — only T0 (teacher-forced) and T1 (self-action open loop, `EVAL_DOCTRINE.md` as amended 2026-09-02); PhysicalAI corpus with `obstacle.offline` agent tracks available **pod-side at train time only** (CLAUDE.md read-set table); v7.2 factored tactical labels (`TACTICAL_LAT_ACTIONS_V7` × `TACTICAL_LON_ACTIONS_V7`, `vocab_v7.py:290-301`); the four metric families with paired episode-cluster bootstrap; the reward/selector disjointness rule (`rl/rewards.py::FORBIDDEN_REWARD_INPUTS`).

### 3.1 The mechanism-level reading that changes the plan

Because the additive branch is zero (§1.3 #2) and both σ floors bind (§1.2 b), **the released RL stage is a policy over two scalars per anchor — a longitudinal scale and a lateral scale of the DDIM mean — trained by a directional score-function gradient under a PDM reward.** Its published gains are exactly where a scale policy can act: **EP +5.3** (stretch along-track where the road is clear), DAC +1.7 (shrink laterally off the drivable edge), and no movement in NC/TTC/Comf. Our fan's deficit is on the same axis: `D-REFCV3-AXIS1` (MEASURED, n = 4,823 / 141 eps) puts **92.2 %** of the `os − ha` gap along-track, with `corr(Δv_plan, Δv_GT)` 0.555 vs hold-action's 0.851, and `D-REFCV4-GATE1` shows the refined fan is a 66 % improvement over the raw bank on the same axis. ⇒ **The V2 lever and our deficit are the same dimension.** This is why the DD-faithful sampler (E-DDA-3) is *not* the gate on the RL idea: a per-anchor (a_long, a_lat) scale policy over refcv4's deterministic fan reproduces the released mechanism without a diffusion chain (E-DDA-3b below). The sampler remains the gate for the *paper's* per-step formulation (γ-discounted chain, Eq. 7) and for any diversity claim.

### 3.3 What our RL reward would be — and which candidates are echo traps

The reward is scored at **train time** on a trajectory the model emitted for a recorded scene, against **replayed** other agents — the same non-reactive assumption NAVSIM makes, and privileged inputs are admissible at train time (*"labels may use ego; inference is vision-only"*, CLAUDE.md). The disjointness rule stands: no term may read any model-produced ranking. Classification by whether the term reads the **ego's own GT future** (an echo of the IL target) or the **environment** (agents, geometry, physics):

| candidate term | reads | verdict | family it serves |
|---|---|---|---|

| "stay within X m of the recorded path" (map-less lane proxy) | ego GT future, banded | ⛔ **ECHO with a dead zone** — the recorded path is the answer | — |

## 4. One page for the PI — what V2 would do to REF-C

**What V2 is.** DiffusionDrive with two extra training stages and a bigger chooser. The encoder and the trajectory generator are the same code and the same weights; V2 (I) fine-tunes only the trajectory head for 10 epochs with a simulator score as the reward, and (II) trains a separate 4-layer scorer that predicts NAVSIM's five sub-scores for 800 candidate trajectories and picks the best. Published: 88.1 → 91.2 PDMS, of which the authors' own control says +1.0 is the chooser and ≈ +2.1 the RL; the weakest candidates in the fan improve far more (PDMS@10 75.3 → 84.4) than the best one (93.5 → 94.9), and the fan gets 28 % narrower.

**What the code says that the paper does not.** The RL stage's "exploration" is two numbers per candidate — stretch-or-shrink along the road, stretch-or-shrink sideways — at 4 % per step; the likelihood used for the update is a fixed 5 m / 2 m Gaussian that does not describe that sampler; a candidate earns positive credit only if it scores at least as well as the human's own trajectory under the simulator, and is punished (−1) for a collision **or** for leaving the drivable area; every candidate is also pulled toward the human's trajectory (weight 0.1, or 1.0 when nothing beat the human); the decoder is one layer, not two; the old confidence head is dead. Nothing in it is a trust region.

**Why that matters for us.** Our fan's measured deficit is along-track speed (92 % of the gap vs hold-action, `D-REFCV3-AXIS1`), and V2's lever is an along-track/lateral scale policy whose published gain is progress (+5.3 EP). **They pull on the axis we are short on.** And because the released mechanism never uses the diffusion chain's randomness, we do not need the DD-faithful sampler (E-DDA-3) to test it — a scale policy over refcv4's deterministic fan is the faithful port.

**Usable now (no sampler):** (1) the selector stage on our emitted fan, with heads for the four families instead of NAVSIM's five (E-DDA-2b, dev box, ~1 day); (2) the ≥ GT bar and the scale-only exploration added to the RL library and re-run on the P-RC21 pilot rig (E-DDA-3b, 4060, minutes per arm); (3) head-only, frozen-trunk RL — already how our library runs.
**Needs the sampler first:** the paper's per-step chain objective (γ-discounted REINFORCE over 10 denoising steps) and any claim about *diversity*, because our fan is deterministic (D-REFC-DDAUDIT-1); that is E-DDA-3, unchanged.
**Does not port:** the map half of the reward (drivable area, lane keeping — a third of V2's gain came from DAC) and the simulator itself; our replacement is collision/TTC/headway against replayed `obstacle.offline` tracks plus comfort and progress, scored train-side only.
**Echo traps to refuse in advance:** any reward that reads the ego's own recorded future — distance to the GT path, speed-profile match, "stay near the recorded lane", route-agreement — is the imitation loss in a reward costume; V2 itself brushes this (progress is normalised by the human's progress) and we should take only the *bar* (beat the human's score), never the *shape*.

**The three experiments that decide it, each pre-registered both ways (§3.4):** E-DDA-3b (does the scale policy shrink the along-track gap without raising collisions?) → E-DDA-2b (does a sub-metric chooser close the 0.075 m selection gap without buying it with collisions?) → E-DDA-6 (is "group by tactical class" at least as good as "group by anchor"?). Only after 3b lands does E-DDA-3 (the real sampler) buy anything V2 has shown.

**What V2 does not settle for us.** Its numbers are NAVSIM's 4-s non-reactive pseudo-simulation — under the 2026-09-02 ruling that is our T1 tier, not closed loop; it has no tactical or strategic metric at all (our v7.2 labels give what NAVSIM lacks); every number is single-run without an interval; and the reward is never ablated.
