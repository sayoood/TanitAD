## Headline

1. **Where the chain breaks: at SELECTION inside the decoder (link C → K), not in generation and not in E9.**
   * **The fan contains a candidate turning the correct way on 100 % of GT-turn windows** (107 windows / 38 episodes;
     the oracle-117 averages ADE 1.19 m).
   * **The decoder's pick turns the correct way on 84.1 % [74, 92]**; a random candidate does so on 38.5 %. E9 changes
     nothing: 84.1 %.
   * **The drop D_core = +0.159 [+0.080, +0.258]** is the only positive drop whose CI excludes 0. It **replicates on
     sampler seed 1** (+0.150 [+0.076, +0.245]) and on the 12 reel clips (+0.373 [+0.014, +0.886], 3 clips with turns).
   * **On heading magnitude (within 15° of GT) the loss is larger:** oracle 75.7 % → pick 51.4 %. The ADE on turn
     windows goes from 1.19 m (oracle) to 3.19 m (pick).
2. **The route signal never reaches the pick, and the tactical head is NOT what carries the turn.**
   * The tactical lat head has the right side on only **39.3 % [27, 52]** of GT-turn windows; it says LANE_KEEP on
     26/40 left and 36/67 right turns. The pick (84 %) is far better than the tactical head.
   * The nav-compliance graft is effectively dead. Its gate is **0.163**, about **4 % of the score spread**, and
     removing it changes **1–2 of 291** informative-nav windows' picks.
   * The nav token is per-clip: **193 of the 291 left/right-nav windows on the eval grid are GT-straight**. A model
     fed that token cannot learn WHEN to turn.
3. **The cheap inference-only levers FAILED. 16 arms in all:** SPEC §5's 7 rules, A1's 4, the A2 linear re-scorer
   (3 arms, plus a shuffled-target control), and the A3 gradient-boosted re-scorer (2 arms). Every arm misses its
   pre-registered bar.
   * The best all-window arm is "E9 goal graft off" (W3): −0.071 [−0.145, −0.015] m. It does **not replicate on seed 1**
     (−0.024 [−0.084, +0.026]).
   * The best turn arm is "nav-compliance ×10" (V3): turn-direction +4.7 pp; turn ADE −0.064 (seed 0, CI includes 0)
     and −0.097 [−0.197, −0.013] (seed 1); it costs +0.024 m on straight driving.
   * Even a 16-feature re-scorer fitted on TRAIN over the model's own selection terms gains only 0.015 m in
     cross-validation and nothing on EVAL. With a linear or a tree-based function class, **the information needed to
     beat the shipped pick is not in these inference-time outputs**.
4. **Label-side bounds rank the next (training) levers by measured size.** These are attribution only, not levers.
   * **Choosing the right SPEED profile** (6 s progress within 10 % of GT) is worth **−0.606 [−0.728, −0.493] m ADE on
     all windows** and −0.892 m on turns.
   * **Choosing the right TURN DIRECTION, perfectly timed,** is worth −0.566 [−0.986, −0.239] m on turns but only −0.076
     m on all windows.
   * So the PI's "not following the route" is real: 16 % of turn windows go the wrong way and the pick under-turns.
     The largest error term even on turns, however, is longitudinal.
5. **Fan:** E9's score ranks the fan well overall. The per-window Spearman of score vs −ADE is 0.753 [0.731, 0.773];
   the random-score control reads −0.005. The "messy" look is the top-8 tail:
   * the top-8 spans 54° of terminal heading on turn windows and 27° on straight ones;
   * the top-8 holds both a left and a right candidate on **27 %** of windows (**30 %** of GT-straight).
6. **Boxes: the clusters are real duplicates, and an inference-time centre-distance NMS removes them.** Fitted on
   TRAIN, scored on EVAL, box3d head:
   * at gate 0.2589, a detected object carries **2.12 boxes on average**, 66 % have ≥ 2, and the duplicates' scores
     differ by a median of only 0.033;
   * **NMS at 2.5 m** brings this to 1.07 boxes per object;
   * **AP@2 m 0.248 → 0.349 (+0.101 [+0.083, +0.121])**;
   * **F1 0.303 → 0.392 (+0.089 [+0.075, +0.105])**, with conf_ratio 0.995 at the TRAIN P = R gate re-fitted after NMS
     (0.2145);
   * the agent head: AP 0.131 → 0.300 (+0.168 [+0.141, +0.193]).
   * The BEV-IoU NMS is worse than centre distance (AP 0.327), because the predicted extents and yaws are too noisy to
     carry the suppression.

**Reading.**
* **Generation is not the problem.** The fan always holds a candidate turning the right way, and at a heading within
  15° of GT.
* **Selection is.** The decoder's rank score is the last place where turn correctness is lost, and it loses it to the
  tune of 16 pp (direction) and 24 pp (heading magnitude) against the oracle.
* **E9 neither helps nor hurts direction.** It changes the pick on 20 of the 107 turn windows but the direction class
  on none of them.
* **The tactical head runs BELOW the plan.** On GT-turn windows it is mostly LANE_KEEP, so the chain does not flow
  nav → tactical → plan at all.
* **The tactical label explains part of that.** On the EVAL grid `lat_v7` is IGNORE on **72 of 107** GT-turn windows,
  and where present it agrees with the 6-s geometric turn side on only **49 %**. The head is trained on a target that
  does not encode "a turn within the next 6 s".

### 1.3 Link (e): the nav-compliance graft

Measured on the 291 EVAL windows of left/right clips:
* the predicate holds for a median of **36 of 117** candidates; no window has zero compliant candidates;
* on GT-turn windows where nav is on the right side (47), the **oracle complies on 93.6 %** and the E9 pick on
  **76.6 %**;
* **learned `navc_gate` = 0.1625**, which is **0.042–0.045× the per-window score std** (median; all / turn windows).
  The median top-1 / top-2 score gap is 0.21 on turn windows and 0.54 on straight ones;
* removing the term changes the decoder pick on **1** window and the E9 pick on **2** of 291.

As a hard filter the predicate would *keep* the GT-like candidate on turns, but on the 193 GT-straight windows of
turn clips it would force a turn (§2). The soft term is inert.

### 1.4 Link (f): E9's score of the GT-like candidate

On the 107 GT-turn windows:
* within `reach_keep`, the oracle's E9 rank has a **median of 3** (p90 14); it is the pick on 24.3 % and in the
  top-8 on 79.4 %;
* the best direction-correct candidate has a median rank of 3;
* the median score gap (pick − oracle) is 0.628.

**Which term prefers the pick over the oracle** (81 turn windows where they differ):
* **the sampler's own confidence favours the pick on 81.5 %** (median Δ +0.99);

* navc 1 % (it is ~0).

On straight windows the oracle is the pick on 50.9 % (median rank 1, top-8 on 94.6 %).

### 1.6 Does the §26.1 ceiling matter here?

**Not for route following.** With the ceiling applied to E9, turn-direction correctness is unchanged (0.841), and
turn ADE moves by −0.011 [−0.073, +0.043].

It does matter for straight driving and overall ADE: the emulated §26.1 fix **worsens all-window ADE by +0.086
[+0.018, +0.158] m**, and seed 1 gives +0.087 [+0.017, +0.160]. On straight windows the signed along-track error at 6 s
moves from +0.43 to −0.11 m, i.e. the fix makes the plan slower than GT.

⚠️ This bears on the R4 restart, where the fix becomes active. Obedience is the PI's requirement (R1), and this is an
open-loop ADE cost of honouring the fed ceiling as binned. It should be read with the ceiling-bin calibration, not as
a reason to drop the fix.

### 2.3 Where this stops (Rule Zero)

**Verdict: no inference-only lever clears the bar. The route-following chain breaks at decoder selection, and every
remaining lever is a TRAINING lever, ranked by its measured bound.** All three are blocked by the PI-binding launch
gate (no training without a launch-gate PASS token). They are unblocked by a Master-Mind SPEC + launch-gate PASS for
the R4 restart or a branch from `ckpt.pt`, each first on the v7-tiny ladder.

1. **R-SPEED, the selector's speed choice.** Bound: −0.606 m all, −0.892 m on turns.
   * Evidence: the sampler's own confidence dominates the rank (A2 weight 3.13; it favours the pick over the oracle on
     81–88 % of disagreements), and the 0–2 s longitudinal κ is 0.54 vs the oracle's 0.85.
   * Lever: train the ranking on the emitted fan with a listwise target. Alternatively build the refcv7 disentangled
     scorer (`refcv7_wta` / `refcv7_select`): it is in the launch code, but **NOT built in this run** (the
     `refcv7_wta_built: false` capture gate).
2. **R-ROUTE-TIME, a time-localised route input.** Bound (B1t): −0.566 m on GT-turn windows, turn direction
   0.84 → 1.00.
   * The labels already carry `nav_30s`: per-clip entries `NAV_TURN_L/R` with `t_start_s / t_end_s` and arc-length
     `distance_m`, anchor-relative. This is the PI's 2026-08-23 "nav with time and distance args". The run feeds only
     the bare token (`nav_args: false`).
   * Same oracle class as the token already fed (ego-future provenance), so it carries the same optimism caveat.
3. **R-TAC-LABEL, a tactical lat target that encodes the next-6-s turn.**
   * `lat_v7` is IGNORE on 67 % of GT-turn windows and agrees with the geometric 6-s side on 49 % of the rest, so the
     tactical head (39 % side-correct on GT turns) cannot carry the turn into `tac8` / behaviour.
   * Labels may use ego (PI 2026-08-03): a kinematic turn label is admissible.
   * Without R-ROUTE-TIME this is the vision-only route to turn timing.
4. **R-PRIOR (lower, n small):** the residual prior `ha0_ext_pose` shares the side of 82 % of the wrong-direction turn
   picks (14/17) and drives the roundabout failure. Candidates are prior dropout in training or a route-aware prior.

**The cheapest experiment that could still have made this work at inference** was a learned re-weighting of the
model's own terms (A2, A3). It was run and it failed (TRAIN CV +0.015 m). Nothing cheaper is left on the selection
