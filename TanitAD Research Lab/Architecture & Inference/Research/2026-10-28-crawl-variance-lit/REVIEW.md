# Inertia / copycat and variance reduction: targeted literature pass for the v8F planner

Date 2026-10-28. Scope: the launch crawl and run-to-run variance of imitation planner O. No GPU, no git, no `kb_add.py`.
Every citation is `[arXiv-id p.N]` (PDF page; PDFs in `pdf/`, hashes in `BANKING_MANIFEST.txt`). Tables were read from `pdftotext -raw` text, not from figures; figure-only results are marked as unread.
Hypothesis cards for the levers: `HYPOTHESES.md` (ids `H-CRAWL-01..09`).

**Evidence classes.** PUBLISHED = primary PDF, page cited. INHERITED = Master Mind number, not re-verified here. ESTIMATED = my arithmetic or judgement, reasoning stated. HYPOTHESIS = untested.

**Our numbers (all INHERITED, 2026-10-28):** launch gap mean(v_own − v_GT) ≈ −0.84…−0.87 m/s over 327 GT-launch windows (T1-kin, open loop); `--launch-intent-w` moved it +0.03 m/s, not separated; same-recipe same-init run pair differs by −0.556 [−0.699, −0.398] m/s; 6 s ADE run-to-run floor ≈ 0.40 m; levers read with 2 seeds × 2 same-seed pairs.
**Constraints:** inference = vision + measured ego history (speed, yaw rate); goal input never carries the situation classifier's output; no steering-angle input.

## 1. Bottom line

1. **No paper measures our quantity.** Every published effect below is a CARLA success rate, nuPlan closed-loop score, inertia-failure count or ImageNet accuracy. Converting any of them to m/s at launch is ESTIMATED, and the cards say so.
2. **The mechanism is real and strong in open-loop planners.** Zeroing the ego-velocity input of VAD collapses its plan to near-stationary (avg L2 6.16 m vs 0.37 m), while blank camera images barely change it (0.46 m) [2312.03031 p.5, Table 2]. A planner that tracks its own input speed crawls from a low-speed start. Whether ours does is a zero-training measurement (`H-CRAWL-02`).
3. **DAgger is not a copycat cure in the literature.** CARLA NoCrash-Dense success: BC with history 33.0±4.2, DAgger with 120k expert queries 35.2±3.1, keyframe reweighting 43.4±0.8 [2106.06452 p.7, Table 1]. A DAgger-trained planner that still crawls is not surprising.
4. **Part of the −0.85 m/s may be outcome conditioning, not a defect.** The 327 windows are selected on the GT future (the human launched). Any predictor that cannot see the trigger is biased low on exactly those windows. This is my inference, not a citation (§5); `H-CRAWL-01` measures it for free and should run first.
5. **Variance:** weight averaging of same-init runs is well founded and costs zero training, but it removes only the run-specific term, never a bias shared by all runs. Published gains are small (+0.7 pp on ImageNet) [2203.05482 p.6].
6. **Floors vs effects.** A same-recipe pair already differs by 0.556 m/s. Every lever I rank has an ESTIMATED effect of 0.05–0.5 m/s, i.e. at or below that floor. With 2 seeds none can be certified unless it lands at the top of its range. The first move is to shrink the floor (averaging, seed pooling), then read levers.

**What A says about *our* crawl (ESTIMATED reasoning).**
- Our history is speed and yaw-rate. Previous acceleration is recoverable from the speed history, so A2's copycat channel exists in our inputs.
- Once speed is an input (as in CARLA-100), copycat fixes gain less: A3 reaches only the BC-SO level, +10 pp over BC-OH.
- The two interventions with the largest *inertia-specific* effect (A9) use an auxiliary stop/go loss and input-state noise, both training-only.
- A published fix for the "launches slower than the human" shape (a continuous bias, not a binary stall) does not exist in this pass. A9 counts 8-s stalls.

## 4. B. Variance reduction for small fine-tuned policies

**B1. Why weight averaging should work here.** Models fine-tuned from the same pretrained weights stay in one basin, close in parameter space [2008.11687 p.1]. Networks become stable to SGD noise early in training, then land in one linearly connected region [1912.05671 p.1]. Weight averaging then acts like ensembling, and it succeeds when the variance term dominates the error [2205.09739 p.1]. Our two runs share an init, so the preconditions plausibly hold. This is ESTIMATED; the barrier test in H-CRAWL-07 checks it.
**B2. Measured effects of averaging.**
- Greedy soup on CLIP ViT-B/32: ImageNet **81.03 vs 80.38** best individual (+0.7 pp, 72 hyperparameter-diverse ingredients). The **uniform soup was 79.97, below** the best individual: it helps only when all ingredients are good [2203.05482 p.6, Table 3 and takeaway 2].
- EMA and SWA improve a single model, and soups still help on top of them [2203.05482 p.30, App. L].
- SWA: +1.3 to +1.9 pp on CIFAR-100 at 1.5× budget (e.g. ResNet-164 80.35±0.16 vs 78.49±0.36) [1803.05407 p.8, Table 1]. This is generalisation, not seed variance.
- LAWA (average the k latest epoch-end checkpoints) speeds progress by dozens of epochs [2209.14981 p.1]. Applies to within-run noise only.
- WiSE-FT interpolation init↔fine-tuned: +1.6 pp ImageNet [2109.01903 p.1].

**B3. Output ensembles.** Deep Ensembles recommend M=5; random init + shuffling suffices, bagging hurt [1612.01474 p.4-5]. Ensembles are slightly better than soups in-distribution (81.19 vs 81.03) and worse out-of-distribution [2203.05482 p.5-6, Fig 4, Table 3]. Cost is M× inference.
**B4. More seeds vs more steps.** Same hyperparameters, different seeds give "substantially different results"; init and data order contribute comparably [2002.06305 p.1]. Codevilla's conclusion: run multiple seeds and pick/pool [1904.08980 p.9]. Given B1 (runs commit to a basin early), more steps inside a run is unlikely to remove between-run differences while pooling K runs divides the run term by ~√K (ESTIMATED; `H-CRAWL-03` tests it with existing checkpoints). Seed-only soups exist in the soups paper (Fig G.4, p.19) but the values are figure-only and unread.
**B5. Checkpoint noise.** Choosing the checkpoint by validation loss or taking the last one is 10-100 % worse than the best online checkpoint [2108.03298 p.7, Fig 4a]. Fix a step budget and report it, or average the tail.
**B6. Driving-specific evidence (thin).**
- CILRS success-rate "variance" over 12 seeds (Empty/Regular/Dense): 4/12/38 % with ImageNet init, 23/26/42 % without; the text says success "can change by up to 42 %" [1904.08980 p.8, Table 3]. The unit is the paper's own; I did not re-derive it.
- TF++ uses 3 training seeds × 3 evals and reports std (e.g. DS 49±8 for waypoints) [2306.07957 p.3, p.6].
- CLOVER on NAVSIM: 3 seeds, PDMS 94.488/94.502/94.492, spread <0.02 over ~12k scenes [2605.15120 p.30-31, Table 21].
- Read together: **aggregate metrics over thousands of scenes have tiny seed spread; narrow or interactive subsets do not.** Our 327-window launch subset is the second kind (ESTIMATED). I found **no driving-planner paper that measures weight averaging or EMA** (one web search for each of: soups + planners, soups + robot policies, EMA/checkpoint averaging + nuPlan/NAVSIM; negative results from search are not proof of absence).

## 5. Gaps and caveats (read before quoting)

- **Outcome conditioning (ESTIMATED, not cited).** The launch gap is computed on windows chosen because the human launched. An outcome-blind predictor, which says "stay" in a stopped scene it cannot read, scores negative on those windows by construction. The comparison that separates a *defect* from this selection effect is the same signed gap on GT-stay windows plus an outcome-blind conditional-median baseline (`H-CRAWL-01`). The P10 floor planner is subject to the same selection; the INHERITED comparison with it should be read with that in mind.
- **Curvature and yaw-rate error at low speed.** No paper in this pass addresses it. Identity: κ̂ − κ = (ω̂ − ω)/v, so a fixed yaw-rate error is amplified as 1/v (ESTIMATED). Part of the "higher curvature error at low speed" may be metric conditioning, the same class as the 2026-09-26 yaw-rate mask retraction. Mask or bin by speed before reading it as policy quality.
- **Single-run evidence.** PlanTF, ChauffeurNet, Cultrera, Li and Dauner report no seeds or replicate runs (no occurrence of "seed" in their text). Their effects cannot be read against a run-to-run floor, and PlanTF's own two no-SDE baselines differ by ~6 NR-CLS points. Treat them as direction, not size.
- **Benchmarks differ from ours.** CARLA success, nuPlan closed-loop and inertia-stall counts are closed-loop. Ours is T1-kin open loop. Open-loop and closed-loop scores are misaligned in nuPlan [2306.07962 p.4], and open-loop planners can win on ego status alone [2305.10430 p.1].
- **Secondary or partial reads.** Wang et al. GTA-V numbers come via de Haan (SECONDARY). Soups Fig G.4 and Codevilla Fig 5 are figures, not read. CIM's table was read from raw text; its architecture is out of scope.
- **Extraction.** Text from `pdftotext`; numbers re-checked against `-raw` output for Tables in A1, A3, A7, A9, A10, B2. Page numbers are PDF pages.
