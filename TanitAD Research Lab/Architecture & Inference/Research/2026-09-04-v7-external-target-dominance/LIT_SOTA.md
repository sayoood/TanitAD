## 0. Verdict in six lines

1. **S1 — there IS a published rule, and gradient-norm balancing is NOT it.** ARML's prior-divergence rule (`lib:2010.08244`) beats grid search in one pass on a supervised+SSL hybrid; GradNorm does not. And in the second hybrid table (`lib:2110.14048` Table 4, CIFAR-10 semi-supervised), a *norm-equalising* method (MGDA) **destroys** the main task: **49.27 vs 67.05** at 500 labels, because it *"significantly overlooks the main classification task"*.
2. **S1 — the mechanism that decides dominance is named and it is measurable in two scalars.** `lib:2505.08170` §3 defines **Gradient Conflict** `⟨g_dist, g_task⟩ < 0` and **Gradient Dominance** `‖g_dist‖/‖g_task‖`, and Fig. 3(b) measures the latter at **10⁻³–10⁰** in a real KD run — the distillation gradient up to **1000× smaller** than the task gradient. That ratio, at our encoder output, is the E-DEC-9 diagnostic.
3. **S1 — a weight is the wrong control variable for a reason that is published, and it is not GradNorm's reason.** `lib:2505.08170` §3: tuning the two α's *"might become extremely tricky as the norm of gradients varies throughout optimization"* — the ratio is **non-stationary**, so any fixed `w` is correct at one step only.
4. **S1 — the strongest counter-evidence in the field is real but does NOT reach our case.** `lib:2201.04122` finds *"no SMTO consistently outperforms unitary scalarization"* — but **every task in every one of its benchmarks has an external target**. None has a self-generated-target term whose optimum is satisfiable with zero scene content. Our E-DEC-7 property is exactly what its benchmarks lack (§3.1).
5. **S3 — subspace separation is measured larger than balancing (MoKD Table 3, +1.3 vs +0.3 AP), and the interference itself is directly measured in the field** (`lib:2001.06782` Defs. 1–2; `lib:2505.08170` Fig. 3).
6. **S2/S4/S5 — see §§ below.** The staged-vs-joint family has a direct primary (`lib:2302.14138`, Layer Grafted) whose stated motivation is that the joint combination fails; the validity-flag family has a clean published fix; the "trivial control beats the learned planner" family has a partial answer and one named absence.

### S1 — the shape of the answer, stated as the programme needs it
* **What determines whether the external term dominates:** the **product** of (a) the gradient-magnitude ratio at the shared layer and (b) the sign of the gradient inner product, both measured *over training*, not at one step. Neither is a loss weight, and neither is currently instrumented in our trainer.
* **Is gradient-norm balancing measured to beat weight sweeping?** ⛔ **In our substrate class, NO** — `lib:2010.08244` Table 1 (GradNorm 14.07 / 7.68 vs GridSearch 13.76 / 6.07) and `lib:2110.14048` Table 4 (GradNorm 81.03 vs fixed-weight ARML 81.35 at 2000 labels). GradNorm's own contrary result is on 3 supervised tasks.
* **What IS measured to beat weight sweeping in this substrate:** ARML's prior-divergence rule (`lib:2010.08244`) and CAGrad (`lib:2110.14048` Table 4: +1.20 / +1.15 / +1.46 accuracy over the fixed-weight baseline at 500/1000/2000 labels).

### S3 — the synthesis the programme should act on
* **Separation is the bigger measured lever** (MoKD +1.3 vs +0.3 AP; SD-JEPA's falsifier makes the split — not the extra loss — the load-bearing part).
* **But the split must be at the level where the fight is.** SD-JEPA's own caveat and SpawnLoRA's `J̄ ≈ 0.056` result agree: a split of the *outputs* leaves the *shared parameters* contended. E-DEC-9's fight is in the encoder ⇒ **an O7-head / O5-head split is NOT the S3 experiment.**
* **A mis-targeted split is worse than none** (SD-JEPA Table 2: 92 vs 96). If v7 splits the latent, every term must be confined to its own block, and the falsifier arm (split present, term applied to the full latent) has to be run.
* **HYPOTHESIS (ours, unproven):** orthogonality protects when capacity is ample (`lib:1912.06844`, full CNN encoder) and fails when capacity is scarce (`lib:2609.03150`, low-rank adapter). If true, the decisive v7 variable is *encoder capacity relative to the two objectives*, not the weight. ⚠️ Marked HYPOTHESIS: no primary tests capacity as the moderator, and the two papers differ in substrate as well as in capacity.

## S2 — SEQUENTIAL / STAGED objectives instead of simultaneous ones

### S2 headline verdict
**This is the best-evidenced search of the five, and it CLOSES a named absence the previous pass declared open.** `…/2026-09-03-v7-issue-audit/LIT.md` §4 item 3 states that *no primary measures that an external-target term at weight `w` fails to prevent a co-trained self-supervised term's collapse*. **`lib:2509.10156` Table 3 is exactly that measurement, it was already in our Library, and it had not been read.** Adding a self-generated-target latent loss to an external-target (pixel) loss as a weighted sum drove SSv2 from **50.1 → 3.7**, and **two different weight schedules both collapsed** — only *staging* (progressive freezing) worked. Three further primaries, on three different objective pairs, reproduce the same shape: **joint co-training of a self-supervised term with another objective loses to running them in sequence, and the ORDER of the sequence matters more than the staging itself.**

## S5 — Validity-flag / masked-input degeneracy

**Our measurement (MEASURED, ours, INHERITED here from the brief):** with `ego_dropout 0.5` and no validity flag, **52.227 %** of training inputs read zero speed, of which **95.737 % were withheld vs 4.263 % genuine (22.5 : 1)**; at eval **100 %** of zeros are genuine.

### S5 — the fix the literature actually supports, in order
1. **Add the explicit observed/validity flag** (`lib:2407.19804` abstract; `lib:2303.03369` prompts). It is the standard, and it is measured to help even under MCAR.
2. **Do not let the withheld value collide with a legal value.** Our zero *is* a legal speed (E-DEC: `refcv3`'s zero-collision defect — a dropped `v0` is indistinguishable from a genuine 0.0 m/s). The published pattern is a distinct token/channel, not a sentinel.
3. **Run the must-move control** (`lib:2608.29005` §4.2): the model's output has to differ between "flag = withheld, value = 0" and "flag = observed, value = 0". If it does not, the flag is inert.
4. **Pre-register the MCAR prediction** (`lib:2407.19804` §2): under a coin-flip dropout the flag's learned weight should be ~0 for a model that was *not* exploiting the code. A large weight is the diagnosis, not the cure.
