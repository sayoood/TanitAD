\section{Theory}\label{sec:theory}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
This section states what the design guarantees and under which assumptions. Every proposition is
elementary; the point is to make the assumptions explicit, because several failures observed in
our own programme (action echo, split-mean bias, uncalibrated imagination) came from unstated ones. A red-team pass over an earlier draft found
that the InfoNCE statement did not cover the masked loss the design uses, that a conformal claim needed an unavailable conditional coverage, and that a future critic was not identified; each is corrected below.

\subsection{Setting}
Let $s\in\mathcal S$ be the multimodal state (frames, ego state, optional audio). A decision is a leaf of a
rooted tree $\mathcal T$ of depth $L$: $a=(v_1,\dots,v_L)$ with $v_\ell\in C(v_{\ell-1})$, where $C(v)$ is the
finite set of children of node $v$ and $v_0$ is the root. In HiCAP $L=3$ (strategic action, tactical cell, trajectory entry;
\S\ref{sec:vocab}); the trajectory entry is a pair (path, profile) drawn from a product, which is scored coarse-then-compose (below). Each node $v$ has a unit-norm embedding $\mathbf a_v\in\mathbb S^{d-1}$
and each level $\ell$ a unit-norm state readout $\mathbf s_\ell(s)\in\mathbb S^{d-1}$. The critic is
$f_\ell(s,v)=\tau\langle\mathbf s_\ell(s),\mathbf a_v\rangle$ with temperature $\tau>0$.
The expert (logged) decision is drawn from $p(a\mid s)=\prod_{\ell}p(v_\ell\mid v_{\ell-1},s)$.

\subsection{What contrastive training estimates, and the prior correction}
\begin{proposition}[Per-node InfoNCE optimum, unmasked]\label{prop:infonce}
Fix a parent $u$ and a state $s$. Draw a positive child $v^+\sim p(\cdot\mid s,u)$ and $K$ negatives
i.i.d.\ from a proposal $q_u$ on $C(u)$ with $\operatorname{supp}p\subseteq\operatorname{supp}q_u$, and remove no candidate from the normaliser. Among all critics $f$, the InfoNCE loss
$-\mathbb E\log\frac{e^{f(s,v^+)}}{e^{f(s,v^+)}+\sum_{k=1}^{K}e^{f(s,v^-_k)}}$
is minimised by
\begin{equation}
f^\star(s,v)=\log\frac{p(v\mid s,u)}{q_u(v)}+c(s),\label{eq:pmi}
\end{equation}
for any function $c$ of $s$ alone.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For fixed $s$ the loss is the cross-entropy of a $(K{+}1)$-way classifier that must identify which of
$K{+}1$ candidates was drawn from $p(\cdot\mid s,u)$ rather than from $q_u$. Its Bayes posterior that candidate $j$ is the positive is
$\frac{p(v_j\mid s,u)/q_u(v_j)}{\sum_i p(v_i\mid s,u)/q_u(v_i)}$, and the softmax over $f$ reproduces this posterior
exactly when $f=\log(p/q)+c(s)$, since $c(s)$ cancels in the normalisation. Cross-entropy is minimised
by the Bayes posterior. This is the noise-contrastive argument of \citet{oord2018cpc} and \citet{poole2019variational}.\qedhere
\end{proof}
\noindent\textbf{Remark (masking changes the optimum; corrected).} CLM additionally removes from each row the negatives that share the positive's action. Then the positive is compared only against \emph{different} actions, the candidates are no longer exchangeable (the Bayes posterior gains a factor the critic $f(s,v)$ cannot represent), and $f^\star$ is no longer the pointwise mutual information. We do not derive the masked optimum. A tabular numerical check on the worked example below (\texttt{RT\_masked\_infonce\_check.py}, \evM) recovers the PMI without the mask (centred critic $(-0.237,0.332,-0.095)$ against $(-0.238,0.322,-0.084)$) and shows that with the mask the critic picks the most frequent action in both states, so that the $\beta=1$ correction over-corrects (cruise probability $0.97$ and $0.99$ against the true $0.60$ and $0.80$); no single $\beta$ repairs it. HiCAP's default loss is therefore the \emph{level-wide soft-target softmax} (\S\ref{sec:train}), a classifier whose optimum is $\log p+c(s)$ with no prior term, in which near-positive nodes receive target mass and are \emph{not} removed from the normaliser. Masked InfoNCE, unmasked InfoNCE and the softmax are compared in H-HC4; for the masked variant $\beta$ is empirical.

\begin{corollary}[Selection bias and its correction, unmasked loss]\label{cor:bias}
If $q_u$ is the marginal $\bar p_u(v)=\mathbb E_{s\mid u}\,p(v\mid s,u)$ (in-batch negatives from batches grouped by parent), then
$\arg\max_v f^\star(s,v)=\arg\max_v\log\frac{p(v\mid s,u)}{\bar p_u(v)}$, the pointwise-mutual-information
maximiser. It \emph{can} differ from the likelihood maximiser $\arg\max_v p(v\mid s,u)$, and only if $\bar p_u$ is not uniform. Adding
$\beta\log\hat q_u(v)$ with $\beta=1$ recovers $\log p(v\mid s,u)+c(s)$. Because $\hat q$ and the critic are
estimated, $\beta$ is fitted on held-out training episodes rather than fixed.
\end{corollary}
\noindent\textit{Example.} Prior $(0.70,0.20,0.10)$ over \{cruise, follow-lead, lane-change\}, true conditional
$(0.60,0.30,0.10)$ in a given state: PMI is $(-0.15,+0.41,0)$, so the PMI argmax is ``follow-lead'' while the most
likely action is ``cruise''. (Under the prior $(0.5,0.3,0.2)$ a conditional such as $(0.9,0.05,0.05)$ gives the same argmax for both: the effect needs a conditional that is not much more peaked than the prior.)
Our corpus is dominated by straight cruising, so the effect is plausible; \S\ref{sec:prelim} gives its measured size on a synthetic world.

\begin{proposition}[Tree factorisation]\label{prop:tree}
Let $\hat p(v\mid u,s)=\operatorname{softmax}_{v'\in K(u)}\big(f_\ell(s,v')+\beta_\ell\log\hat q_u(v')\big)_v$ over a non-empty kept set $K(u)\subseteq C(u)$ (if the rule set empties it, fall back to the top-scoring child). Then
$\hat p(a\mid s)=\prod_{\ell=1}^{L}\hat p(v_\ell\mid v_{\ell-1},s)$ is a normalised distribution over the
retained leaves. By the chain rule the marginal of a node is the product along its path and equals the sum of the leaf
probabilities beneath it. Two distributions coexist in HiCAP: the conditional product $\hat p$, used by the decision rule, and the level-wide marginals $\tilde p_\ell$ used to rank nodes across parents; they are separate parameters, tied by a KL term (\S\ref{sec:train}), not equal, and the conformal keep-sets of rule R6 are calibrated on $\tilde p_\ell$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each factor is a softmax on a non-empty set of children of one node, so it sums to one; the product over a root-to-leaf
path therefore sums to one over the retained leaves by induction on depth.\qedhere
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Marginal node score]\label{prop:marginal}
Fix a level $\ell$ with node set $V_\ell$ and a beam of width $w$. The probability that the beam contains the ancestor of the expert's action is
$\sum_{v\in\text{beam}}P_\ell(v\mid s)$, where $P_\ell(v\mid s)=\sum_{a\in\mathrm{subtree}(v)}p(a\mid s)$ is the marginal mass of the node. It is maximised over all beams of width $w$ by the $w$ nodes of largest \emph{marginal}, and no node score that is not monotone in the marginal can do better at that level.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The expert's action falls in the subtree of exactly one node of the level, so the retention probability of a set of nodes is the sum of their marginal masses; the maximum over sets of size $w$ is attained by the $w$ largest terms.\qedhere
\end{proof}
\noindent This is why level critics are trained as marginals with level-wide negatives (\S\ref{sec:train}): a sibling-only softmax leaves a per-(state, parent) offset free, and a mean-pooled child centroid scores the average child, neither of which is monotone in the marginal. Two cautions: retaining the ancestor of the single \emph{most likely} leaf would call for a max-heap score instead (the expert's action is a draw from $p$, so the marginal is the right target here), and per-level optimality does not compose across levels (a parent of mass $0.5$ spread over ten children beats a parent of mass $0.4$ on one child at a width of one, yet retains only $0.05$ of the leaf mass against $0.4$); a cascade-optimal score would use the best mass reachable under the downstream widths.

\begin{proposition}[Beam miss under noisy level critics]\label{prop:beam}
Let a level have $N$ children, let the true ancestor have score margin $m_j$ over competitor $j$, and let node scores carry independent errors of standard deviation $\sigma$. With beam width $w$ the ancestor is lost only if at least $w$ competitors outscore it, and
\begin{equation}
P_{\text{miss}}\;\le\;\frac1w\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\bar\Phi\!\Big(\frac{m_j}{\sigma\sqrt2}\Big),
\end{equation}
with $\bar\Phi$ the standard normal tail. Over $L$ levels the ancestor path survives with probability at least $1-\sum_\ell P_{\text{miss},\ell}$. To keep level $\ell$ at miss $\le\alpha_\ell$ it suffices that $w_\ell\ge(N_\ell-1)\,\bar\Phi\big(m_\ell/(\sigma_\ell\sqrt2)\big)/\alpha_\ell$ when the margins are equal.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The difference of two independent errors is normal with variance $2\sigma^2$, so competitor $j$ outscores the ancestor with probability $\bar\Phi(m_j/(\sigma\sqrt2))$. The number of outscoring competitors has this expectation summed over $j$, and Markov's inequality gives $\Pr(\text{count}\ge w)\le\mathbb E[\text{count}]/w$. The union bound over levels gives the survival statement.\qedhere
\end{proof}
\noindent The width is thus a proxy for critic quality: a critic that delivers margin $m$ at noise $\sigma$ buys a narrower beam, which is the rationale for a beam width that follows a calibrated margin rather than a constant.

\subsection{Cost and memory of hierarchical retrieval}
\begin{proposition}[Retrieval cost]\label{prop:cost}
Let level $\ell$ have branching $B_\ell=\max_u|C(u)|$ and beam width $b_\ell$ ($b_0=1$). Flat scoring of
$N=\prod_\ell B_\ell$ leaves costs $Nd$ multiply-accumulates and streams $N\,d\,w$ bytes of embeddings ($w$ bytes per element)
per decision. Beam retrieval touches $M=\sum_{\ell}b_{\ell-1}B_\ell$ nodes, i.e.\ $Md$ multiply-accumulates and $Mdw$ bytes.
For a product vocabulary of $K_P$ paths and $K_V$ profiles ($N=K_PK_V$ composed entries), scoring the factors separately, keeping $(k_P,k_V)$ and composing costs $M=K_P+K_V+k_Pk_V$ rows. This selects the best composed entry \emph{exactly} only if the composed score is separable, $f(p,q)=f_P(p)+f_V(q)$; with a bounded pair interaction $|b(p,q)|\le B$, keeping every path within $2B$ of the $k_P$-th path score and every profile within $2B$ of the $k_V$-th profile score makes the composition exact (a threshold-algorithm argument). Because HiCAP's fine stage adds a pair head, the recall of composition is otherwise \emph{measured}, not assumed (H-HC3).
\end{proposition}
\noindent For $1{,}024\times256$ entries with $(20,10)$ kept this is $1{,}480$ rows instead of $262{,}144$, a factor of $177$ (Table~\ref{tab:cost}). On an edge device the binding constraint is memory bandwidth, not arithmetic (\S\ref{sec:eff}), and at this size flat scoring is itself affordable ($\approx1$\,ms), so the case for the hierarchy is the small set the backbone can reason over and the level-wise priors, and only beyond a few million entries the speed.

\subsection{Recall under cascaded pruning}
Pruning is safe only if it rarely removes the right action. We give two mechanisms.
\begin{proposition}[Conformal cascade]\label{prop:conformal}
For level $\ell$ let $\rho_\ell(s,v)$ be any score and $\hat t_\ell$ the $\lceil(1-\alpha_\ell)(n+1)\rceil$-th smallest value of
$-\rho_\ell(s_i,v_i^\star)$ over $n$ calibration units, each scored against the children of \emph{its own logged parent}. For a test window let
$K^\star_\ell(s)=\{v\in C(v^\star_{\ell-1}):\,-\rho_\ell(s,v)\le\hat t_\ell\}$ be the keep-set that would be used if the logged parent had been retained.
If calibration and test units are exchangeable, then $\Pr\big(v_\ell^\star\in K^\star_\ell(s)\big)\ge 1-\alpha_\ell$ for each level. The cascade retains the logged leaf exactly when
$v^\star_\ell\in K^\star_\ell(s)$ at every level, hence
\begin{equation}
\Pr\big(\text{logged leaf retained}\big)\;\ge\;1-\sum_{\ell}\alpha_\ell .\label{eq:recall}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Level-wise coverage is the split-conformal guarantee \citep{angelopoulos2021gentle}: the test nonconformity $-\rho_\ell(s,v^\star_\ell)$ is exchangeable with the calibration values, so it is at most their $\lceil(1-\alpha_\ell)(n+1)\rceil$-th smallest with probability at least $1-\alpha_\ell$.
If the logged ancestors were all retained, the cascade's candidate set at level $\ell$ is the set of children of the logged parent, thresholded at $\hat t_\ell$, i.e.\ $K^\star_\ell(s)$; so the logged leaf is retained iff no level misses.
The union bound over the $L$ events $\{v^\star_\ell\notin K^\star_\ell(s)\}$, each of probability at most $\alpha_\ell$, gives \eqref{eq:recall}.\qedhere
\end{proof}
\noindent We state the union bound only: a product $\prod_\ell(1-\alpha_\ell)$ would require coverage \emph{conditional on} the parent having been retained, which conditions on an event of the test window and is not covered by exchangeability. Cascades of conformal sets with a summed miss budget are known \citep{fisch2021cascade}.
Three cautions follow from the measurements of \S\ref{sec:prelim} and the red-team pass. (a)~\emph{The exchangeable unit is the episode}, not the window: windows within an episode are strongly dependent, so calibrate with one window per episode or with cluster (episode-level) conformal, on episode-disjoint splits. (b)~The guarantee is marginal over calibration draws: conditional on one calibration set, coverage follows a Beta$(k,n{+}1{-}k)$ law whose median is the nominal level, so about half of all draws fall below nominal at any $n$ even with exact exchangeability; a guarantee \emph{per calibration draw} needs the training-conditional (PAC) form, which picks the quantile index $k'$ so that $\mathrm{BetaCDF}(1{-}\alpha;k',n{+}1{-}k')\le\delta$ \citep{vovk2012conditional}. (c)~Marginal coverage hides rare classes, so keep-sets are calibrated per tactical class (Mondrian).

\begin{proposition}[Physical mask]\label{prop:mask}
Let $\mathcal B$ be a set of kinematic limits (longitudinal acceleration and braking, lateral acceleration) and
$\mathcal K_{\mathcal B}(s)=\{a:\ \text{the implied path satisfies }\mathcal B\text{ given the current speed}\}$. If the logged
path satisfies $\mathcal B$, the mask retains the \emph{logged future} with probability one. The oracle-in-set error over the candidate set is unchanged if and only if the oracle-best \emph{candidate} is feasible; a sufficient condition is that the limits are widened by the feasibility functional's Lipschitz constant times the quantisation radius of the vocabulary.
\end{proposition}
\noindent That the mask retains the logged future is a statement about the bounds, not about the network, so it is checked empirically rather than proved.
Section~\ref{sec:prelim} reports that physical limits keep $881/881$ logged 2\,s futures of the validation set
(one-sided 95\% lower bound $0.05^{1/881}=0.9966$ on the window-level retention rate if windows were independent) while removing
$78\%$ of a speed-agnostic anchor set, and that the oracle-in-set error is unchanged to three decimals (the direct evidence for the second claim). Tight comfort limits ($3/6/4$\,m/s$^2$ for acceleration, braking, lateral) are \emph{not} safe as hard masks
(they removed $2.4\%$ of logged futures) and are therefore used as costs. The accelerations are $0.5$\,s finite differences of four waypoints, which low-pass the peaks: the test is \emph{lenient} (it under-estimates peak acceleration and errs toward retaining), not conservative for feasibility.
The 40 validation episodes make the effective sample smaller than the window count, which is why the calibrated conformal mask, not this bound, is the shipped guarantee.

\subsection{Predictive imagination that is identifiable}
A world model trained on expert logs cannot learn what happens under an action the expert never took,
because in the log the action is a function of the state. We therefore split the outcome into a part the ego cannot
change over the horizon and a part it controls.
\begin{proposition}[Identifiable outcome factorisation]\label{prop:ident}
Let $W_{t+1:t+H}$ be the future scene (other agents, signals) and $e_{t+1:t+H}$ the planned ego path. If no component of the scene is causally downstream of the ego action over the horizon (non-reactivity), then $p\big(W_{t+1:t+H}\mid s,\mathrm{do}(a)\big)=p(W_{t+1:t+H}\mid s)$, which is identified from the log by marginalising over the logged action; the observational independence $W\perp a\mid s$ is neither required nor implied (an expert acting on cues absent from $s$ can break it without reactivity, and it can hold under reactivity by cancellation). The planned ego path $e=\kappa(a,s_{\text{ego}})$ is a known function; tracking error lies outside the proposition. Any interaction cost $c(W,e)$ can then be evaluated for every candidate $a$ without a learned counterfactual model.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Non-reactivity means the scene is a non-descendant of the action in the causal graph, so intervening on $a$ leaves its conditional law given $s$ unchanged, and $p(W\mid s)$ is a functional of the observational joint distribution obtained by marginalising the logged action. The planned ego path is a deterministic function of the candidate and the ego state.\qedhere
\end{proof}
\noindent The assumption fails for reactive agents (a driver yielding to the ego). We do not hide this: reactivity is
bounded by a learned correction term whose weight is limited and which is reported separately, and imagination is
never allowed to \emph{raise} a candidate's standing except through a learned benefit gate and a disagreement veto (\S\ref{sec:imag}).

\begin{proposition}[Outcome critics: what is and is not identified]\label{prop:future}
Let $g(s,a,x)=\tau\langle\Phi(s,a),\mathbf z(x)\rangle$ be trained with InfoNCE over \emph{outcomes} $x$ (negatives are outcomes of other windows). Its optimum is $g^\star=\log\frac{p(x\mid s,a)}{p(x)}+c(s,a)$, a density ratio of the dynamics, but only up to an arbitrary function $c(s,a)$ of the candidate. Hence $g$ can rank outcomes for a fixed $(s,a)$ but \emph{cannot rank candidates at a fixed state}. A candidate-ranking critic must normalise over candidates for a realised outcome, a softmax over the per-state counterfactual set, whose optimum is $\log\frac{p(a\mid s,x)}{q(a\mid s)}+c(s,x)$, and this requires counterfactual data.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The first statement is Proposition~\ref{prop:infonce} with the positive drawn from $p(x\mid s,a)$ and negatives from the marginal $p(x)$: the softmax normalises over $x$ for fixed $(s,a)$, so any $c(s,a)$ cancels. The second statement is Proposition~\ref{prop:infonce} with the roles of candidate and outcome exchanged.\qedhere
\end{proof}
\noindent Consequently HiCAP never ranks candidates by an outcome-InfoNCE score. Outcome InfoNCE is used only as auxiliary representation shaping, with targets in the current ego frame; candidate costs come from cost heads trained on counterfactual costs where those exist (\S\ref{sec:imag}).

\subsection{Distilling the selector into a small student}
\begin{proposition}[Decision preservation, single-level argmax]\label{prop:distill}
Let student embeddings satisfy $\|\hat{\mathbf s}-\mathbf s\|_2\le\delta_s$ and $\|\hat{\mathbf a}-\mathbf a\|_2\le\delta_a$ (unit vectors).
Then $|\tau\langle\hat{\mathbf s},\hat{\mathbf a}\rangle-\tau\langle\mathbf s,\mathbf a\rangle|\le\tau(\delta_s+\delta_a)$, and the student's
arg\,max equals the teacher's whenever the teacher's top-1/top-2 score margin exceeds $2\tau(\delta_s+\delta_a)$. With shared action embeddings ($\delta_a=0$) it suffices that the margin to each competitor $v$ exceeds $\tau\,\delta_s\,\|\mathbf a_{\text{top}}-\mathbf a_v\|\le2\tau\delta_s$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\langle\hat{\mathbf s},\hat{\mathbf a}\rangle-\langle\mathbf s,\mathbf a\rangle=\langle\hat{\mathbf s}-\mathbf s,\hat{\mathbf a}\rangle+\langle\mathbf s,\hat{\mathbf a}-\mathbf a\rangle$,
each term bounded by Cauchy--Schwarz. A rank change between two candidates requires their scores to move toward each other by
at least the margin, i.e.\ each by at least half of it. With $\delta_a=0$ the change of the gap between two candidates is $\tau\langle\hat{\mathbf s}-\mathbf s,\mathbf a_{\text{top}}-\mathbf a_v\rangle$, bounded by $\tau\delta_s\|\mathbf a_{\text{top}}-\mathbf a_v\|$.\qedhere
\end{proof}
\noindent The guarantee covers the single-level dot-product argmax only. For the cascade it must hold at each level's $b$-th/$(b{+}1)$-th boundary and at each conformal threshold, and it does not cover the re-score, the cost heads or imagination. At the CLM temperature cap $\tau=100$ it requires $\delta_s<\text{margin}/200$, so it is close to vacuous unless margins are large; H-HC9 measures the share of real windows that meet it. Because the interface between backbone and heads is a fixed-dimension embedding space, the frozen
multi-billion-parameter backbone can, if a latency tier fails, be replaced by a smaller student trained to match the cached teacher embeddings and
candidate scores, and Proposition~\ref{prop:distill} converts an embedding-error budget into a decision statement for that argmax. Under the size ruling of \S\ref{sec:latency} the student is an option, not a requirement.
