\section{Knowledge, explanation and audio in one embedding space (v10)}\label{sec:v10}
Four requests of the PI (2026-10-25) extend the design without changing its control path: supervision for imagination from the corpus's own agent tracks, traffic-rule knowledge retrieved by the country the vehicle is in, the country itself as a strategic label, an audio encoder bridged into the backbone's embedding space, and the backbone's reasoning text as an optional output. They share one idea, and the section states it first. The design specification with the data probes behind every count is \texttt{Paper/HiCAP/design/HICAP\_V10\_SPEC.md}.

\subsection{One shared embedding space}\label{sec:shared-space}
Every non-visual source enters HiCAP in the frozen backbone's \emph{text-embedding space} of width $d_v$ ($2{,}048$ for the Cosmos3-Edge reasoner, $4{,}096$ for the 8\,B tier). Node definitions already did (\S\ref{sec:vocab}); we add the country nodes (the text path's last-token state on ``driving in $\langle$country$\rangle$: $\langle$summary$\rangle$''), one embedding per traffic rule, and the output of a trainable audio bridge aligned to the text path's embedding of an event caption. This is the pattern of omni-modal models, in which an audio encoder is projected into the language model's embedding space (Qwen2.5-Omni and Qwen3-Omni; Gemma~3n, \evP{} from the model cards, encoder identities re-verified in research note R-G), applied here to a backbone that stays frozen. The consequence for HiCAP is that one mechanism consumes every such source: a zero-initialised gated cross-attention read from the level queries into a set of $d_v$ tokens,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf q_\ell' = \mathbf q_\ell + \tanh(g^{\text{src}}_\ell)\;\mathrm{CrossAttn}\big(\mathbf q_\ell,\ W^{\text{src}}\,\mathcal T^{\text{src}}\big),\qquad g^{\text{src}}_\ell\equiv0\ \text{at initialisation},
\label{eq:gated-memory}
\end{equation}
with $\mathcal T^{\text{src}}$ the audio tokens or the retrieved rule tokens. A source that is absent, or a gate at zero, leaves the model bit-exactly unchanged, so every source is an ablation with a deterministic null, and adding one never disturbs an earlier result.

\subsection{Traffic-rule knowledge and the country as a strategic label}\label{sec:rules}
\paragraph{The label.} The upstream PhysicalAI-AV release records the country of every clip (\texttt{metadata/data\_collection.parquet}, $306{,}152$ clips, $25$ countries). Joined to our corpus by clip identifier it covers $4{,}369/4{,}369$ train and $139/139$ evaluation clips \evM; the train split is close to balanced, from $68$ clips (Slovenia) to $278$ (Denmark), with $265$ from the United States, and the evaluation split has $23$ countries, five of them with a single clip. The country is therefore a clip-level label available at no labelling cost, which v10 references rather than copies (\S\ref{sec:v10-data}).
\paragraph{Where it is learned.} The strategic query $\mathbf q_{s1}$ gets a contrastive country readout against the country node embeddings, with the critic temperature of \S\ref{sec:train} and weight $\lambda_c=0.1$. The cues are visual (signs, road markings, number plates, road furniture), and the readout produces a posterior $p(c\mid\cdot)$ every tick. A supplied country (a GNSS geofence, which a production vehicle has) is an alternative input arm; the predicted arm is primary.
\paragraph{The RuleBook.} Rules are versioned records $\{\text{id},\ \text{countries},\ \text{text},\ \text{arguments},\ \text{road type},\ \text{effect},\ \text{source}\}$, append-only like the node library, and restricted to what changes a driving decision: default speed limits per road type, turning right on red (permitted after a stop in the United States unless signed; in Germany only at the green-arrow sign; otherwise prohibited in the European countries of the corpus), priority to the right at unsigned junctions, yielding on entry to roundabouts, yielding to emergency vehicles, yielding at pedestrian crossings, tram priority, the United States school-bus stop and mandatory daytime lights. Every record cites an official source or is marked unverified. Each rule text is embedded once by the frozen text path, $\mathbf r_i\in\mathbb R^{d_v}$.
\paragraph{Retrieval and the three routes.} Per tick, the membership weight of rule $i$ is $w_i=\sum_c p(c)\,\mathbb 1[i\in\mathcal R_c]$, its relevance to the scene is $s_i=\langle W_q\mathbf q_{s2},\,W_r\mathbf r_i\rangle$, and the $k=8$ rules with the largest $w_i\,\mathrm{softmax}(s)_i$ are retrieved. They are used along the three knowledge routes of the design: as \emph{memory} through Eq.~\eqref{eq:gated-memory} into the strategic and tactical queries; as \emph{pruning}, where rules with numeric arguments become masks after the physical-limit mask (a speed-profile cap at the limit with a tolerance when the road type is known; right-on-red candidates removed where prohibited), never removing the guard set; and as \emph{verification and explanation}, where the retrieved texts join the System-2 verifier's prompt and the explanation prompt (\S\ref{sec:explain}). Retrieval costs one $d_v$-dimensional score per rule of the current country, negligible beside one fusion layer. The knowledge modules add $6.10$\,M trainable parameters (country readout $3.67$\,M, gated read $2.10$\,M, retrieval projections $0.33$\,M), bringing the complete model to $64.37$\,M before the audio bridge \evM. RuleBook~v1 holds $106$ records for the $25$ countries, $73$ of them confirmed by a search restricted to official domains and $33$ unverified; no official page could be read verbatim from our environment, so every record is to be checked against its cited statute before H-HC19.
\paragraph{Admissibility.} The binding goal-input rule of the programme forbids the situation classifier's output inside the goal input. HiCAP has no situation-classifier head; the country posterior is not a situation class; and the goal and route path never reads it (the rule memory updates the level queries, not the goal token). The leak test of the vision-only rule applies to the label: it comes from upstream metadata, which is an inference input only in the supplied arm, and both arms are reported.
\paragraph{What the corpus can and cannot test.} All $25$ countries drive on the right, so the driving side is untestable here; right-on-red differs only on the $265$ United States clips, and the corpus has no traffic-light labels, so a right-on-red effect is a hypothesis until a labelled set exists \evH; speed-limit compliance is testable through the longitudinal family against the release's per-clip speed limit input. The registered tests are country accuracy against the majority and against chance ($1/25$), per country with its $n$ (H-HC18); rules on against rules off, paired, per family (H-HC19); and a \emph{country-swap counterfactual}, which replaces the posterior by another country's and requires the decision to change only on rule-relevant cells (H-HC20), a specificity test whose failure would show the memory acting as a generic bias rather than as rules.

\subsection{The backbone's reasoning as an optional output}\label{sec:explain}
The reasoner can explain what HiCAP chose. After selection, the decision is rendered as text (strategic node, tactical cell, target speed) together with the retrieved rule texts, and the frozen reasoner decodes at most $48$ tokens from the \emph{key--value cache of the latest refresh prefill}, so no second vision pass is run. The channel runs at most every two seconds inside the refresh duty budget ($D\le0.5$, \S\ref{sec:latency}) and is one-way: nothing it produces enters the control computation, which a test checks by comparing the command with the channel on and off. A consistency monitor embeds the explanation with the same frozen text path, maps it to the nearest tactical node, and logs disagreement with the executed node; letting that disagreement raise the verifier's trigger is a registered ablation, not the default, because it would put generated text into the control loop.
Zero-shot prompting is the default. An optional low-rank adapter on the reasoner's text channel only (switched off during tap extraction, so the cached features are byte-identical) can be trained on the chain-of-causation texts the corpus already carries: Alpamayo2-Super outputs for $4{,}729$ clips at one anchor ($t_0=5.1$\,s) with a chain-of-thought, a meta-action and an auto-labelled chain of causation, and NVIDIA's out-of-distribution chain-of-causation set, of which $50$ clips overlap the corpus \evM. These texts are a teacher's output, not ground truth. Measured at the aligned v9 row, the teacher's meta-action agrees with the v9 longitudinal class on $0.427$ of clips ($95\%$ interval $[0.413,0.442]$, $\kappa=0.255$, $n=4{,}363$) and with the lateral class on $0.855$ ($\kappa=0.314$, $n=3{,}803$), and it names fewer than a quarter of the v9 turns \evM; part of the longitudinal gap is a horizon mismatch (the teacher describes the immediate future, v9 the $[2,8]$\,s band). The texts are therefore unusable as tactical supervision and usable for explanations only after filtering. The PI asked for reasoning at every frame. Re-checked on 2026-10-26 across every file, branch and commit of the augmentation release and every other programme dataset: the run has exactly one anchor per clip ($t_0=5.1$\,s), and its trajectory task stored only the shape and error of the predicted trajectory, not its coordinates \evM; and a dense regeneration of the $35.8$\,B teacher over all $896{,}942$ rows is a pod job whose cost is set out in the runbook before it is launched. The metrics are the text-to-node agreement rate, the decision-consistency rate and a human audit sample; no text-overlap score is reported alone.

\subsection{Audio: an encoder and a bridge into the shared space}\label{sec:audio-bridge}
\paragraph{No programme corpus has audio.} Measured on 2026-10-25: the $36$ upstream features of PhysicalAI-AV are two ego-motion streams, the obstacle tracks, six calibration tables, seven cameras, one LiDAR and nineteen radars, none of them a microphone; and the shipped video container of the corpus holds one HEVC video stream and no audio stream ($2/2$ files probed) \evM. The last open check of \S\ref{sec:audio} is thereby closed: audio has to come from outside the corpus.
\paragraph{Design.} A frozen audio encoder reads a two-second log-mel window every $0.5$\,s. The default is the Qwen3-Omni audio encoder ($647.9$\,M parameters \evM{} from its weight headers, Apache-2.0, about $6.8$\,ms per window on Thor \evE), with BEATs as the contender, decided by the tower probe E-N0. The omni models themselves bridge with a projector of only $2.4$--$4.6$\,M parameters scattered into the language model's input embeddings (Qwen2.5-Omni, Qwen3-Omni, Gemma~3n, Gemma~4; \evM{} from their configurations); ours is a perceiver because it must also pool to a fixed eight tokens and accept array features for bearing; a trainable bridge, a two-layer perceiver with eight latent queries followed by an MLP, maps it to eight tokens in $\mathbb R^{d_v}$. The tokens are consumed by Eq.~\eqref{eq:gated-memory} (the default, which keeps the backbone cache intact) or appended to the frozen backbone's prefill as soft tokens (the native, omni-style ablation of \S\ref{sec:audio}).
\paragraph{Training in three steps.} (A1)~Bridge alignment with the backbone and HiCAP frozen: a symmetric InfoNCE loss plus a cosine term between the pooled bridge output and the frozen text path's embedding of the caption, on public audio--caption and strongly tagged sets (driving-relevant classes: sirens, horns, train horns and crossing bells, reversing beepers, tyre squeal, impacts), each with its licence recorded. (A2)~Event heads (class, bearing with a microphone array, distance bin, approaching) from event metadata only. (A3)~HiCAP's audio gate on synthetic events rendered from \emph{real agent tracks} of the corpus (delay, $1/r$ gain, Doppler, absorption, interaural differences, ego-noise floor) and supervised through knowledge rather than trajectories: the event's caption embedding retrieves the rule ``yield to emergency vehicles'', so no logged trajectory is asked to react to a sound it never heard, which avoids the label echo of \S\ref{sec:audio}. All seven audio controls remain mandatory, audio-off is every family's primary number, and audio-on is a paired arm. The bridge and event heads have $10.13$\,M parameters at $d_v=2{,}048$ (mono) \evM; with the gated read widened to $d_v$ the complete model is $74.62$\,M trainable. No siren or horn alignment set we could reach is cleared for commercial use; the PI ruled on 2026-10-26 that this is a research programme and research licences suffice, so A1 uses them, with each set's licence recorded.

\subsection{Supervision for imagination from the corpus's agent tracks}\label{sec:imag-supervision}
The corpus ships the upstream obstacle tracks (\texttt{agents/obstacle\_offline}: $4{,}585$ of $4{,}719$ clips, $29.4$\,M cuboid rows; $134$ clips have no source file and six have no cuboid \evM). From them and the ego poses, a producer computes per trainer row: the lead agent (the nearest track overlapping the ego's logged future corridor within $80$\,m along the path), the Tier-1 targets of \S\ref{sec:imag} (gap, closing speed and lead acceleration at $0.5$, $1$, $2$ and $3$\,s, a gap uncertainty target, the mode among cruise, brake, accelerate and cut-out with cut-ins flagged, and the onset quantiles), and a three-channel teacher cost for any candidate trajectory (time to collision of its swept footprint against the agents' logged futures, minimum clearance, and progress deficit), which shapes the Tier-0 targets and supervises the Tier-2 heads. The release's own per-row lead gap and time gap serve as an independent cross-check. The cost is open-loop and non-reactive: logged agents do not respond to a counterfactual ego, which is the standard caveat of log-replay benchmarks and the reason imagination stays a target shape, an expectation and a veto rather than a planner.

\subsection{What v10 adds to the data, by reference}\label{sec:v10-data}
v10 is the v9 label release plus the unchanged v8.1 strategic labels plus new components keyed by clip identifier: the country (built, $4{,}508$ clips), the imagination supervision (producer written, sample built), the RuleBook and its embeddings, the chain-of-causation texts aligned to the trainer clock, and a reference table to the upstream chunks of the six cameras the corpus does not ship (elastic sensing needs them; they are pulled on the training pod, never copied into the release). All seven cameras exist upstream for every one of the $4{,}508$ clips, spread over $1{,}363$ chunks; pulling whole chunks would move $11.5$\,TB \evM, reading only our clips' members about $384$\,GB \evE.
