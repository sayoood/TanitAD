\section{Imagination that can be trusted}\label{sec:imag}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Prediction structure and imagination are the natural way to give a selector a sense of consequence: rather than asking ``which candidate resembles what the expert did'', ask ``what happens if I do this''.
The obstacle is identification. A world model trained on expert logs sees each action only in the states where the expert chose it, so its answer for any other action is an extrapolation.
Our programme measured two consequences of trusting such extrapolations: a rollout can be perfectly self-consistent for a candidate that is implausible, and rollout confidence can rise while rollout fidelity falls\evI.
HiCAP therefore imagines only what the data can identify (Proposition~\ref{prop:ident}) and uses the result one-sidedly.

\paragraph{Factorised outcome.}
For a candidate leaf $a$ the outcome is $(W,e_a)$: the future scene $W$ (other agents, over the horizon) and the ego path $e_a=\kappa(a,e_t)$, a \emph{known} function of the leaf and the ego state. Only $W$ needs to be learned, and under the non-reactive assumption of Proposition~\ref{prop:ident} it does not depend on $a$.
Crucially, $W$ is represented as \emph{agent tracks in the current ego frame}, not as future camera frames: a future frame contains the ego's own motion and would smuggle the action into the target, breaking the very independence the proposition needs.

\paragraph{Components.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Scene forecast head.} From the fusion tokens (trainable motion tokens included, because frozen tokens lack metric range) a set decoder predicts $K_W=16$ agent slots (position, velocity, existence, covariance) at $0.5,1,2,4,6$\,s. Labels are the logged 3-D agent tracks (privileged, label side, available for $96.83\%$ of training clips\evI), matched to slots by the Hungarian algorithm. One forecast is computed per tick and shared by all candidates.
\item \textbf{Interaction cost (analytic).} $c_A(W,e_a)$ combines minimum time-to-collision and minimum clearance between $e_a$ and the forecast tracks, integrating the forecast covariance; it is evaluated only for the pruned survivors ($|K|\le32$).
\item \textbf{Contrastive future critic.} $g(s,a,s')=\tau\langle\Phi(s,a),\mathbf z(s')\rangle$ with $s'$ an embedding of the realised future \emph{agent set} encoded by a small set encoder, trained with in-batch InfoNCE against other windows' futures; Proposition~\ref{prop:future} says its optimum is a pointwise mutual information between candidate and outcome. Mapped to a cost by isotonic calibration on held-out episodes it gives the second head $c_B$.
\item \textbf{Reactivity correction.} A bounded term $\Delta_{\text{react}}(s,a)\in[-r_{\max},0]$ lets the cost of a candidate fall when agents would plausibly yield; its weight is limited by $r_{\max}$ and it is reported as a separate row, because the assumption it patches is the one most likely to fail.
\end{enumerate}
\begin{equation}
c_{\text{img}}(s,a)=\gamma(s,a)\,\max\!\Big(0,\ \tfrac12\big(c_A+c_B\big)+\Delta_{\text{react}}\Big),\qquad
\gamma(s,a)=\mathbb 1\big[\,|c_A-c_B|\le\eta\,\big],
\end{equation}
with the agreement tolerance $\eta$ calibrated on held-out training episodes. The decision rule of \S\ref{sec:train} adds $\lambda_{\text{img}}c_{\text{img}}$ to the cost. Two design choices follow directly from the failure modes above: imagination can only \emph{add cost}, never subtract it, so it cannot make a candidate look better than the base score does; and when the two independent heads disagree it has no effect, so a rise in confidence cannot come from a single head extrapolating. The forecast head is also what lets the elastic controller of \S\ref{sec:elastic} read the number and closeness of agents as a cheap cue.

\paragraph{Loss and cost.}
$\mathcal L^{\text{img}}=\mathcal L^{\text{forecast}}+\mathcal L^{\text{nce}}_{\text{future}}+\mathcal L^{\text{cal}}$ (slot-set loss, InfoNCE and calibration). At run time the added cost is one set decoder per tick and $|K|\times K_W\times5$ closed-form evaluations, which is negligible next to the backbone.

\paragraph{What could go wrong, and how the protocol sees it.}
(i)~The forecast may be poor because the backbone features lack range; the failure is visible as a low-quality forecast and no longitudinal gain (H-HC8). (ii)~The programme's earlier attempt to train an agent detector jointly with the trunk failed a two-seed capacity gate at $17$\,M parameters and the deranged-join control reproduced the whole degradation\evI; here the head sits on a frozen backbone and does not compete with a trunk for capacity, but that is a hypothesis. (iii)~The non-reactive assumption fails at exactly the interactions that matter (yielding, merging); the one-sided form limits the damage, and the reactivity row and the imagination-shuffled control (NC7) measure it.
